# Review cycle 1 — full reviewer reports

## R1-novelty-prior-art — Major revision

Novelty and prior-art review, cycle 1. Section 2.3 is candid that the pseudo-count mixture "is not new as a mechanism". The literature search confirms this. Close precedents exist for the mechanism: credibility/Dirichlet-prior regularisation of the empirical score distribution (Zhang, Lu, Yang arXiv:2410.02561, cited as [28]), regularised-Mondrian threshold shrinkage (Rafe & Das arXiv:2605.05562; its abstract confirms it "shrinks group thresholds toward the global quantile"), n0-interpolated site/pooled calibration (Shahid arXiv:2606.20115), and a donor+local mixture for unseen solar stations (Jeon, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.24002627, published 6 Nov 2026). What is left is (i) an empirical benchmark for new building loads and (ii) leave-one-building-out (LOBO) selection of kappa. The paper's own results weaken both. The two refinements that make up the "hierarchical" part of the method's name add nothing. The one methodological difference from [29], selecting kappa by LOBO, is less reliable than the fixed-kappa donor-local mixture: on BDG2 the mean absolute coverage deviation (MACD) is 0.018 for the tuned method against 0.015 for fixed kappa, and fixed kappa is better per building with Holm p = 4.7e-24. The "most reliable" claim also does not hold against the regularised-Mondrian competitor in every setting. Several directly relevant prior works are missing: transfer conformal inference (Zhang et al., JMLR 2026), Posterior Conformal Prediction (Zhang & Candes), localized conformal prediction (Guan 2023), and Neural Conformal Control. [29] is cited as a code record, while the paper it accompanies, under review at Scientific Reports, is not cited, and the description of [29] could not be checked against the record. No patent prior art was found. A benchmark of calibration strategies for newly metered buildings, with per-building coverage, LOBO tuning and transfer tests, appears genuinely unoccupied in the energy literature; the cited Hao et al. [14] could not be retrieved (MDPI returned 403). That benchmark is a publishable applied contribution, but only if it is framed as such and the method branding and headline claims are brought in line with the ablation and fixed-kappa evidence. No new experiments are needed beyond re-tabulating results that already exist and one optional LOBO run with a reliability-weighted objective.

## R2-methodology — Major revision

Methodology review, cycle 1. The code matches the manuscript's equations: Eq. 1 scale (features.py:61-65), Eq. 2-3 scores and interval (pipeline.py:179-184, 253-259), Eq. 5 mixture with the +1 point mass (calibration.py:206-207), the kappa=inf switch to the finite-sample-corrected pooled quantile (calibration.py:201-204), stage bins (calibration.py:27), LOBO with self-exclusion (pipeline.py:200-202; calibration.py:139-143, 169-170), the ACI edge rules, GHCP weights, QShrink and the 0.0066 MACD noise floor. There is no leakage of own scores: stage h uses only stages up to h-1 (calibration.py:193-209). The main problem is in what the paper claims, not in the code. The stage-resolved results files show that the similarity (kNN) prior, which sits inside the headline method HSC-CQR, makes coverage clearly worse in the first onboarding days. The paper instead says these refinements 'added nothing'. LOBO selected the kNN prior in every run, and target results also contradict its kappa choice, which weakens contribution 3 (LOBO-selected pseudo-count). How own scores enter the mixture (pooled across all earlier stages) is inconsistent with the stage-matched prior and with the Discussion's explanation of why stage matching does not matter. The remaining items are minor implementation and reporting mismatches. These issues can be fixed by revising the claims, changing the headline configuration and adding a few diagnostics; the core finding (shrinkage towards own scores beats pooled, ACI, TQA and GHCP calibration) survives.

## R3-experimental-design — Major revision

Experimental-design review, cycle 1. The protocol is broadly sound. Splits are building- and period-disjoint, all calibration-layer methods share one forecaster, one score set and one grid, the tuning tables are stored in results/run_info.json, and the table numbers match the result files I checked (for example T_robustness.csv noise15: SCP-stage under_rate = 1.0, HSC-CQR = 0.017). Four design problems weaken the central claims. (1) The manuscript calls the study pre-registered, but several baselines and variants were added after interim results on target buildings had been seen. (2) The tuned pseudo-count is selected on an objective that is almost flat in kappa and does not measure reliability. An untuned kappa of 168 h beats it on the primary reliability endpoint, and the operational recommendation rests on a sensitivity sweep run on target buildings. (3) The ACI competitors are handicapped by the 500·s radius cap combined with a mean-based tuning objective. (4) The claim that HSC is the most reliable strategy is not robust against QShrink-CQR outside the main protocol, and the stress and transfer results have no paired tests. None of these is fatal. Fixing them takes a few added runs that only re-tune or re-calibrate, plus some rewording.

### R3.1 [Major] 'Pre-registered' claim is overstated: design changes were made after interim target-building results
- Evidence: Section 4.4 says: 'All settings were fixed in a pre-registration file before any full experiment was run.' The abstract says 'pre-registered ... protocol'. EXPERIMENT_LOG.md records: 'Interim (superseded) observations used only for design decisions ... component ablation showing no benefit of kNN prior/stage matching'. REVIEW_HISTORY lists a 'First full-run look (seed 0 BDG2)'. CHANGELOG 2026-11-16, written after that interim run, adds GHCP-CQR, QShrink-CQR, TQA-B-CQR, the HSC simple variants, CQR/HSC-CQR (MLP), HSC-CQR fixed-κ (κ=168) and the extended ACI γ grid. None of these is in experiments/PREREGISTRATION.md, whose baseline list ends with 'HSC on CQR scores (variant)'. The 'simple' variant that the highlights and conclusion recommend was therefore conceived after its components had been seen to fail on target data.
- Action: Replace the sentence with an accurate statement. Add a deviation table (item, date, trigger, whether target results had been seen). Label the post-hoc baselines and variants, and the 'one pseudo-count suffices' conclusion, as exploratory. Qualify 'pre-registered' in the abstract.
- Experiment: None.

### R3.2 [Major] Kappa is selected on a near-flat objective that does not measure reliability, and the recommended value comes from target buildings
- Evidence: run_info main_BDG2_s0_a0.1, tune_hsc_cqr_simple: objective 0.5956 at κ=336, 0.5965 at κ=168 and 0.6002 at κ=∞. That is a 0.15 % difference between candidates, so the selection is close to arbitrary. The objective is a pooled mean of IS/s, while the paper reports medians. Table 5: the untuned fixed-κ variant (168 h) reaches MACD 0.015 with 1.9 % under-covered, against 0.018 and 3.4 % for the LOBO-tuned HSC-CQR. In the paired test, the tuned variant is worse in coverage deviation (r_rb = -0.45, p < 0.001). Sections 6.2 and 6.3 and the conclusion then recommend a pseudo-count of 'about one week' / 'one to two weeks' from Fig. 9, which the paper itself labels 'target buildings ... not used for any choice'.
- Action: Either justify the IS objective as the primary choice and drop the reliability-based κ recommendation, or add a selection rule that targets reliability on calibration buildings and report its target performance. Examples: minimise LOBO MACD, or minimise IS subject to a LOBO under-coverage share ≤ x.
- Experiment: Re-run the LOBO tuning on calibration buildings with a MACD or under-coverage objective (or the constrained IS rule) for HSC-CQR simple and QShrink-CQR on BDG2 and UCI (5 seeds, calibration only). Report the selected κ and the target metrics, and check whether the LOBO-optimal κ matches the target-optimal 96-168 h.

### R3.3 [Moderate] ACI and TQA competitors are handicapped by implementation choices rather than by their mechanism
- Evidence: aci_quantiles (src/models/calibration.py:231) sets the radius to the 500·s grid cap whenever α_h ≤ 0. With the mean-IS objective this makes tuning collapse to tiny γ: tune_aci_cqr (BDG2 s0) objective is 0.224 at γ=1e-3 and jumps to 1.507 at 2e-3. Even the tuned ACI-CQR produces catastrophic intervals: rel_IS maximum 183 on BDG2 and 336 on UCI versus 17.6 and 8.7 for HSC-CQR, and mean 2.37 vs 0.55 on UCI (raw_results.csv.gz). TQA-B's decay (0.8) is fixed, whereas HSC, QShrink and ACI each get a LOBO grid search, so the tuning budget is unequal.
- Action: Add standard safeguards and equalise tuning budgets. Discuss ACI's result as conditional on the empty/infinite-set convention.
- Experiment: ACI-CQR with α_h clipped to e.g. [0.005, 0.5], or AgACI [21] / quantile tracking, with γ tuned by LOBO, on BDG2 and UCI (5 seeds). Tune the TQA-B decay over {0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95} by LOBO.

## R4-statistics — Major revision

Statistical review, cycle 1. I recomputed the per-building paired differences from results/raw_results.csv.gz and they match statistics.csv. For example, HSC-CQR vs CQR on BDG2 CD gives n=713, mean diff -0.0204 and 84.0% better, as in Table 4. The main contrast against pooled CQR is robust: HSC-CQR is better at all 10 BDG2 sites. Several other inferential claims are not, or are misdescribed.

1. The ablation tests show that stage matching and kNN make BDG2 reliability significantly worse, yet the text says they 'add nothing' or fall 'within seed s.d.'.
2. The headline numbers come from the tuned full model, but the paired tests favour the simpler fixed-kappa variant.
3. The Wilcoxon p-values treat 713 buildings as independent, while 31% of them come from one site. Some 'p<0.001' claims, such as the one against Self-CQR, disappear at site level.
4. Table 4 pairs mean-difference CIs with median-type tests, and the two disagree in at least one row.
5. The Holm families are not the ones the caption describes.
6. Stress-test text leaves out significant cases where a competitor beats HSC-CQR.

These are fixable by re-analysis and rewording. No new experiments are needed.

### R4.1 [Major] The ablation is misreported: stage matching and kNN prior make HSC-CQR significantly worse on BDG2
- Evidence: Sec. 5.4 says removing the similarity prior or stage matching 'changes the MACD by less than its seed-to-seed standard deviation', and Highlight 5 says these elements 'add nothing'. results/statistics.csv (BDG2, random, alpha 0.1, abs_dev) shows that the full model is worse in each paired test: HSC-CQR vs '-stage' mean diff +0.00041, r_rb -0.18, p_holm 1.4e-4; vs '-kNN' +0.00051, r_rb -0.16, p_holm 2.8e-4; vs 'simple' +0.00064, r_rb -0.19, p_holm 7.3e-5. For the absolute-score HSC on UCI, removing kNN raises the deviation significantly (HSC vs '-kNN' abs_dev mean diff -0.0059, r_rb 0.52, p_holm 6e-13), so kNN helps there. On BDG2, HSC vs '-kNN' is not significant after Holm (p_holm 0.045 with mean diff -0.00074 and a CI that includes 0). None of these tests appears in the manuscript.
- Action: Report the ablation paired tests in a table (supplementary or Table 5). State that on BDG2 the two refinements significantly reduce per-building reliability (small effect), and that kNN helps the absolute-score HSC on UCI. Rephrase 'add nothing' as 'do not help and slightly hurt the CQR-score variant'.
- Experiment: None; re-analysis of the existing statistics.csv.

### R4.2 [Major] The headline method and numbers are not the most reliable variant according to the paper's own tests
- Evidence: The abstract and conclusion attach '3.4%' and '0.018' to the full HSC-CQR and call it 'the most reliable strategy'. HSC-CQR fixed-kappa beats it on CD on BDG2 (Table 4: +0.0026 [0.0015, 0.0036], r_rb -0.45, p<0.001). My site-level recomputation shows fixed-kappa better at 10/10 sites. HSC-CQR simple is also significantly better (p_holm 7e-5). The LOBO objective (interval score) therefore selects a kappa that is measurably sub-optimal for the paper's stated target, per-building coverage. ## R7-computational — Minor revision

The calibration layer is cheap in principle: a 4000-point grid CDF mixture with a vectorised quantile search, and a store of about 20 MB for about 170 calibration buildings.
