# R1 Novelty and prior art, cycle 2: Major revision

## Recommendation
Major revision.

## Summary
The revision deals honestly with cycle-1 items A1–A3 and B7. It drops "hierarchical", credits the mechanism to [20–22,50], and reports both fixed-κ and IS-tuned rows. Web searches up to Nov 2026 turned up no paper that already does the building-load onboarding comparison. The closest work is still [18–22] plus SGCP (below). The contribution is now empirical: which calibration strategy keeps per-building intervals reliable, and how to tune it. That makes the agreement between claims and per-week numbers the central question, and it does not yet hold. The recommended estimator is also prior art and is matched or beaten by another prior-art estimator.

## Issues

**1. Critical: the 56-day headline hides under-coverage after week 1, and the text says the opposite.**
*Evidence.* Sec 6.3 says the layer "keeps the share of buildings with clearly unreliable intervals small after the first week". Sec 6.1 says own errors "dominate after one to two weeks". T_weeks_BDG2 shows PCS-CQR under-covered (<0.85) for 19.4% of buildings in week 1, 19.8% in week 2, 15.3% in weeks 3–4 and 11.0% in weeks 5–8. UCI: 12.2%, 8.6% and 4.4% for week 2, weeks 3–4 and weeks 5–8. From results/raw/raw_results_main.csv.gz (α=0.1), 41.2% of BDG2 building×seed units under PCS-CQR are under-covered in at least one of weeks 2, 3–4 or 5–8 (CQR 48.6%). UCI: 23.6%. The 1.4% headline is coverage summed over the whole window, so under- and over-coverage in different weeks cancel. That is the same pooling-hides-failures argument the paper makes against pooling across buildings.
*Action.* Remove or qualify the "after the first week" claims. Put per-week under-covered shares in the abstract and conclusion next to the window figure. Define reliability for a time window that matches the decision horizon, for example weekly.
*Experiment.* None new. Re-tabulate from the stored raw results.

**2. Major: the sampling-noise reference assumes independent hours, which the data contradict.**
*Evidence.* Sec 5.1 states "fewer than 0.00% of buildings would fall below 0.85". That is an unformatted value, and it rests on independent misses. Sec 5.5 concludes "the first-week failures are real" from a 1.7% floor for independent misses. Hourly misses cluster within days, so the effective sample size is closer to days than to hours. Seen against that floor, PCS's 11–20% per-week rates would also count as "real". Yet the coverage correlation between weeks 3–4 and weeks 5–8 is −0.10 for PCS-CQR, against +0.47 for CQR (BDG2; UCI −0.08 vs +0.68; my computation from raw). So the residual under-coverage after shrinkage does not persist within a building. It looks like noise, or like over-correction, not like a stable building-specific error.
*Action and experiment.* Build a null that respects autocorrelation. Options: a day-block bootstrap of a building calibrated by construction (miss indicators resampled in daily blocks), or an effective sample size from the day-level autocorrelation of misses. Restate the week-1, per-week and "not sampling artefacts" claims against that null.

**3. Major (novelty): the recommended layer is prior art, and another prior-art estimator matches or beats it.**
*Evidence.* Rafe and Das [21] proposed QShrink. In Table 3, QShrink-CQR (MACD) has a lower under-covered share than PCS-CQR in 13 of 17 rows, ties in 2 and is higher in 2: random 1.3 vs 1.4, LOSO 2.0 vs 2.4, UCI→BDG2 3.4 vs 4.2, UCI 0.2 vs 0.4, outage 3.0 vs 3.6. QShrink is also lower on site-balanced LOSO (1.3 vs 1.7), week 1 (17.7 vs 19.4 on BDG2; 9.3 vs 10.3 on UCI) and booking failures (1.4 vs 2.1). The only advantage of PCS is the BDG2 interval score (−0.0085, p=0.041, about 1.5%), which does not appear on UCI. Even so, Fig. 1, Sec 3.4, the abstract and the Sec 6.3 operating rule are all built around PCS. Separately, Dunn et al. [18] already propose a shrinkage ("borrowing strength") conformal method for new observations in groups that already have data, which is the onboarding case. The paper cites [18] only for unseen groups.
*Action.* Recommend the shrinkage family, or the simpler QShrink, rather than PCS specifically. Credit [18]'s shrinkage method in Sec 2.2. State plainly that both shrinkage estimators exist in the literature, so the contribution is the evaluation and the tuning rule.

**4. Major: the ACI conclusion rests on a grid-boundary γ, and ACI is never compared at matched width.**
*Evidence.* The coverage-only ACI selects γ=0.01, the top of GAMMAS (src/training/pipeline.py:30), in every run (T_tuning). ACI (MACD) selects 0–0.001. Targets are never evaluated at γ=0.002 or 0.005. ACI (MACD) is the best strategy in weeks 5–8 (BDG2 7.1% vs 11.0% for PCS; UCI 3.2% vs 4.4%). So "ACI matched the reliability only with much wider intervals" and "no strategy of comparable width repaired the first week" have not been tested along the ACI trade-off curve.
*Action and experiment.* Run ACI-CQR at every grid γ on the stored scores (cheap). Report the target-side curves of width against under-coverage, overall and for week 1. Compare with PCS at matched median width, and consider combining ACI with shrinkage. Soften the claims if the curves overlap.

**5. Moderate: the conclusions overstate the role of tuning.**
*Evidence.* The conclusion says "What mattered was how the shrinkage was tuned". But IS-tuned vs reliability-tuned PCS differ in under-covered share only at p=0.164 (BDG2: 2.9 vs 1.4%). UCI: 1.1 vs 0.4%, MACD 0.014 vs 0.013. The dominant effect is shrinkage versus none (18.2% → 2.9%). The 2% guard-rail barely binds: in T_tuning_curves, every κ≥24 lies within 2% of the minimum in seeds s0–s3, so the rule is in effect min-LOBO-MACD. "Varies by only 1.2%" is a median. In main_BDG2_s4 the curve varies 6.7% across finite κ (0.7595 at κ=24 vs 0.7120 at κ=336).
*Action.* Say that shrinkage itself is what matters, with tuning a second-order refinement that is significant only for coverage deviation on BDG2. Describe the rule as MACD minimisation, and report the range of the objective as well as the median.

**6. Minor: literature and wording carried over from cycle 1.**
*Evidence.* Cycle-1 item 8 asked for three references, and none was added: Zhong M, Tai W, Zhong T, Zhou F, "Stochastic Grouping Conformal Prediction for Effective Subgroup Reliability", arXiv:2610.11957 (NeurIPS 2026); Zaregarizi and Yavari, arXiv:2605.29733 (2026; cold-start transfer for buildings with uncertainty quantification); and Fong and Holmes, "Conformal Bayesian computation", NeurIPS 2021. Few-shot per-task calibration (Park, Cohen, Simeone, meta-XB, arXiv:2210.03067) is also missing. The highlight "restores reliability" overclaims, given Issue 1 and the 84.8% under-coverage under volatility. The text gives "1344 observed hours" in Sec 5.1 but "≈1300" in Sec 4.4.
*Action.* Add and position these works. Fix the highlight, the "0.00%" artefact and the inconsistent hour count.
