# Results {#sec:results}

All shares in this section use one unit, the building: a building's metrics are averaged over the seeds in which it was a target ({{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_under}} thus means that this share of the 837 BDG2 target buildings is under-covered). "Under-covered" means coverage below 0.85 at the 90 % target. The *oracle reference* is the share expected for a building whose intervals are perfectly calibrated but whose misses have the observed day-to-day dependence (Section [-@sec:design]); it is the same for every method.

## Pooled calibration leaves many new buildings unreliable

The global LightGBM forecaster reaches a median CV(RMSE) of {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_lgb}} on new BDG2 buildings over their first 56 forecast days (seasonal naive: {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_naive}}; MLP: {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_mlp}}) and {{point_UCI_cvrmse_lgb}} on UCI clients (naive {{point_UCI_cvrmse_naive}}; MLP {{point_UCI_cvrmse_mlp}}). The question is whether the intervals of such a forecaster can be trusted building by building.

They cannot when calibration is pooled (Table [-@tbl:main]). Conformalised quantile regression (CQR) leaves {{x_BDG2_CQR_under}} of the BDG2 buildings and {{x_UCI_CQR_under}} of the UCI clients under-covered over the 56-day window, against an oracle reference of {{x_BDG2_ref_win}} and {{x_UCI_ref_win}}. Its mean coverage is nevertheless close to nominal ({{main_BDG2_CQR_mean_cov}} on BDG2). Direct quantile regression with a neural network does not solve the problem (MLP-QR: {{main_BDG2_MLPQR_under_rate}} on BDG2), and the Gaussian MLP lowers it ({{main_BDG2_MLPGauss_under_rate}}) only by over-covering on average ({{main_BDG2_MLPGauss_mean_cov}}) at a median interval score of {{main_BDG2_MLPGauss_rel_IS}} instead of {{main_BDG2_CQR_rel_IS}}. Part of the pooled excess is a uniform level offset between the calibration period and the target period ({{off_BDG2_offset}} in coverage on BDG2); removing it would lower the CQR share by only {{off_BDG2_drop}} percentage points, so most of the excess is genuine heterogeneity between buildings. Pooled under-coverage is concentrated in buildings that are hard to forecast (Spearman correlation of coverage with CV(RMSE): {{ea_BDG2_rho_CQR}} on BDG2), and it is not a borderline effect: {{th_BDG2_CQR_under_5pt}} of the BDG2 buildings miss the target by more than five points (Fig. [-@fig:cdf]).

## Building-adaptive calibration over the onboarding window

All strategies in Table [-@tbl:main] use the same quantile models and CQR scores; they differ only in how a new building's own scores enter the calibration, and every parametrised strategy was tuned by the same constrained leave-one-building-out rule on calibration buildings. The recommended configuration, recency-weighted score shrinkage (HSC-CQR recency), was chosen by the nested rule of Section [-@sec:design] on BDG2 and ranked first in each confirmation setting (UCI, unseen sites, both cross-dataset directions); it was selected in {{sel_boot_primary}} of bootstrap resamples of the selection.

Over the window, the layer leaves {{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_under}} of the BDG2 buildings and {{x_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_under}} of the UCI clients under-covered, below the oracle reference. In the confirmatory comparison with pooled CQR (Supplementary Table S16), the paired difference is {{cf_BDG2_CQR_under_diff}} percentage points on BDG2 (95 % site-cluster bootstrap CI {{cf_BDG2_CQR_under_ci}}; {{cf_BDG2_CQR_under_better}} buildings move from under-covered to covered, {{cf_BDG2_CQR_under_worse}} the other way; Holm-adjusted {{cf_BDG2_CQR_under_p}}) and {{cf_UCI_CQR_under_diff}} points on UCI (CI {{cf_UCI_CQR_under_ci}}). The median relative interval score is equivalent to pooled CQR's within a 2 % margin on both datasets (TOST; ratio {{cf_BDG2_CQR_log_rel_IS_ratio}}, CI {{cf_BDG2_CQR_log_rel_IS_ci}} on BDG2).

The gain is a property of the *shrinkage family*, not of one member. Quantile shrinkage with recency (QShrink-CQR recency: {{x_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_recency_under}} and {{x_UCI_QShrinkCQR_recency_under}}) and without it (QShrink-CQR (cov): {{x_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_cov_under}} and {{x_UCI_QShrinkCQR_cov_under}}), score shrinkage without recency ({{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_simple_cov_under}}) and with a fixed one-week pseudo-count ({{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_fixedkappa_under}}) all remove most of the pooled excess. Against QShrink-CQR (cov), the recommended layer is better over the window on BDG2 ({{cf_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_cov_under_diff}} points, CI {{cf_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_cov_under_ci}}, {{cf_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_cov_under_p}}) but not on UCI ({{cf_UCI_QShrinkCQR_cov_under_p}}), and the two are tied in the first week on both datasets. Online level control tuned by the same constrained rule (ACI-CQR (cov): {{x_BDG2_ACICQR_cov_under}} and {{x_UCI_ACICQR_cov_under}}) and self-calibration with recency (Self-CQR recency: {{x_BDG2_SelfCQR_recency_under}}) are close behind over the window. Strategies that never give the new building more weight than one reference building or bound their adjustment by design, GHCP-style pooling ({{x_BDG2_GHCPCQR_under}}) and temporal quantile adjustment ({{x_BDG2_TQABCQR_under}}), stay close to pooled CQR: they secure validity across buildings but cannot correct a building whose errors are systematically larger than average.

The window gains survive a more conservative information set. With the building's own scores used one day later than in the main protocol (delay 1d, i.e. day $h$ uses scores up to day $h-2$), the layer leaves {{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_delay_1d_under}} (BDG2) and {{x_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_delay_1d_under}} (UCI) under-covered. They also hold for a different forecaster: on the MLP quantile models, the layer reduces the share from {{main_BDG2_CQR_MLP_under_rate}} to {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_MLP_under_rate}} (BDG2) and from {{main_UCI_CQR_MLP_under_rate}} to {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_MLP_under_rate}} (UCI).

## Weekly reliability: what the window metric hides {#sec:weekly}

A window metric averages over eight weeks, and an operator who settles weekly sees something else (Fig. [-@fig:weekly]). In the first week, pooled CQR leaves {{x_BDG2_CQR_under_wk1}} (BDG2) and {{x_UCI_CQR_under_wk1}} (UCI) of the buildings under-covered. The recommended layer reduces this to {{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_under_wk1}} and {{x_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_under_wk1}} (confirmatory differences {{cf_BDG2_CQR_under_wk1_diff}} and {{cf_UCI_CQR_under_wk1_diff}} points, CIs {{cf_BDG2_CQR_under_wk1_ci}} and {{cf_UCI_CQR_under_wk1_ci}}), which is at the oracle reference for one week of data ({{x_BDG2_ref_week}} and {{x_UCI_ref_week}}). Quantile shrinkage achieves the same first week ({{x_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_cov_under_wk1}}, {{x_UCI_QShrinkCQR_cov_under_wk1}}); online level control does not ({{x_BDG2_ACICQR_cov_under_wk1}}, {{x_UCI_ACICQR_cov_under_wk1}}), and self-calibration is worst ({{x_BDG2_SelfCQR_recency_under_wk1}} on BDG2) because a few dozen own scores give noisy quantiles.

From the second week on, the picture is less favourable for every strategy. On BDG2, {{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_wk_under_late}} of the buildings are under-covered in an average week of weeks 2–8, against {{x_BDG2_CQR_wk_under_late}} with pooled CQR and an oracle reference of {{x_BDG2_ref_week}}. The excess over the reference is {{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_excess_wk2_8}} points (CI {{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_excess_wk2_8_ci}}) for the layer and {{x_BDG2_CQR_excess_wk2_8}} points for CQR. On UCI, the layer is at the reference ({{x_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_excess_wk2_8}} points, CI {{x_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_excess_wk2_8_ci}}). Over eight weeks, {{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_anyweek_80}} of the BDG2 buildings have at least one week below 0.80 (CQR {{x_BDG2_CQR_anyweek_80}}). Week-to-week coverage of a single building is inherently noisy: the oracle building itself has some week below 0.85 with probability {{x_BDG2_ref_anyweek}}. The calibration layer therefore removes *systematic* per-building under-coverage, which is what the window metric measures, but weekly reliability on BDG2 remains a few points worse than ideal for all strategies studied.

## The reliability–sharpness frontier {#sec:frontier}

Fig. [-@fig:frontier] maps every strategy family over its full parameter grid on the target buildings. It serves transparency only; no selection was made on target buildings. Over the window (panels a, b), shrinkage with or without recency, quantile shrinkage and ACI at a suitable step size ($\gamma$ = 0.002: {{fr_BDG2_ACI_0.002_under}} on BDG2) all bring the under-covered share close to zero without loss of sharpness. Two differences remain. First, ACI does so only in a narrow band of step sizes: at $\gamma$ = 0.005 the median interval score rises by {{fr_BDG2_ACI_0.005_ISdelta}} on BDG2, at $\gamma$ = 0.01 by {{fr_BDG2_ACI_0.01_ISdelta}}, while the shrinkage families degrade gracefully over almost two orders of magnitude of $\kappa$. Second, in the first week (panels c, d), no ACI step size that preserves sharpness brings the BDG2 share below {{fr_BDG2_ACI_0.002_wk1}}, whereas score shrinkage reaches {{fr_BDG2_HSC_decay_0.95_48_wk1}} at $\kappa$ = 48 h.

The first-week gain comes from a small pseudo-count, not from recency. At equal $\kappa$ = 48 h, the first-week share on BDG2 is {{mk_BDG2_under_wk1_rho0.95_k48}} with recency ($\rho$ = 0.95) and {{mk_BDG2_under_wk1_rho1.0_k48}} without it. Recency acts later in the window, where pre-change and early scores would otherwise dominate the building's own contribution: at $\kappa$ = 48 h, the window share is {{mk_BDG2_under_rho0.95_k48}} with and {{mk_BDG2_under_rho1.0_k48}} without recency. The decay factor was fixed at 0.95 per day and not tuned; $\rho$ = 0.8 lies on a slightly better point of the frontier ({{mk_BDG2_under_rho0.8_k24}} at $\kappa$ = 24 h). Leave-one-building-out selected $\kappa$ = {{tuned_BDG2_hsc_cqr_recency}} h on BDG2 and {{tuned_UCI_hsc_cqr_recency}} h on UCI across seeds.

## Which design elements matter {#sec:ablation}

Fig. [-@fig:ablation] isolates the design elements. *Shrinkage* is indispensable: the same layer with $\kappa=\infty$ (HSC-CQR recency κ=∞) behaves like pooled CQR ({{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_kappa=∞_under}}). *The selection objective* matters next. Selected by the interval score, which is almost flat in $\kappa$, the layer without recency used $\kappa$ = {{tuned_BDG2_kappa_simple}} h and left {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_simple_under_rate}} under-covered; selected by the constrained coverage objective it used {{tuned_BDG2_hsc_cqr_simple_cov}} h ({{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_simple_cov_under}}). *Recency* then lowers the remaining window share (Section [-@sec:frontier]).

Three refinements do not help. A similarity-localised population distribution with onboarding-stage matching (HSC-CQR: {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_under_rate}}) adds nothing over the stage-agnostic simple variant; after scale normalisation and history-aware features, the remaining differences between buildings' score distributions appear to be driven by events rather than by load shape. Stage-wise studentisation alone (SSC-CQR: {{main_BDG2_SSCCQR_under_rate}}) leaves many buildings under-covered: its stage-wise guarantee is a statement about the average over new buildings, not about each building, and it does not even hold here because the calibration and target periods differ (Section [-@sec:method]). Combined with shrinkage (HSC-SSC-CQR), studentisation is competitive in medians ({{x_BDG2_HSCSSCCQR_under}}; first week {{x_BDG2_HSCSSCCQR_under_wk1}}) but fails in the tails: a few buildings with a large running scale receive intervals up to {{tl_BDG2_HSCSSCCQR_max}} times as wide as pooled CQR's, and its mean interval score is {{tl_BDG2_HSCSSCCQR_meanIS_delta}} above CQR's.

## Sharpness tails

Equal medians do not imply equal tails. The recommended layer's mean relative interval score is {{tl_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_meanIS_delta}} above pooled CQR's on BDG2 ({{tl_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_meanIS_delta}} on UCI), its 99th-percentile width ratio is {{tl_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_p99}}, and {{tl_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_n5x}} building-seed units receive intervals more than five times as wide as CQR's (maximum {{tl_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_max}}×). They arise because the building's own scores enter the radius directly: a few extreme own scores widen the interval until recency weighting down-weights them. Capping the radius at the pooled 99 % score quantile (HSC-CQR recency (capped)) removes them (maximum width ratio {{tl_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_capped_max}}×, mean interval score {{tl_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_capped_meanIS_delta}} vs CQR) at a window share of {{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_capped_under}} instead of {{x_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_under}}. Quantile shrinkage ({{tl_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_cov_max}}×), ACI ({{tl_BDG2_ACICQR_cov_max}}×) and self-calibration ({{tl_BDG2_SelfCQR_max}}×) have larger extremes. We recommend the capped variant for deployment.

## Robustness and generalisation

Table [-@tbl:robust] stresses the strategies on BDG2. In all fourteen conditions, the recommended layer keeps the window share below 1 %, and it is significantly lower than ACI-CQR (cov) in thirteen and than QShrink-CQR (cov) in ten of them (paired McNemar tests). Pooled calibration from clean calibration buildings breaks down when the new buildings' meters are noisy ({{rob_noise15_CQR_under_rate}} under-covered at 15 % noise), drift ({{rob_ramp+40_CQR_under_rate}} under a ramp) or shift in level; the layer is largely unaffected because the building's own scores enter the mixture. Two stresses remain hard for every strategy. A level shift in the first two weeks raises first-week under-coverage to about 40 % for all methods, and a gradual ramp leaves about a fifth of the buildings under-protected (upper bound exceeded in more than 10 % of hours) even with the layer, because a ramp biases the point forecast faster than any calibration of a fixed-width radius can follow.

## Operational consequences and computational cost

Table [-@tbl:operational] translates the intervals into day-ahead capacity-headroom offers planned on the upper bound. With pooled CQR, {{tl_BDG2_CQR_underprot}} of the BDG2 buildings and {{tl_UCI_CQR_underprot}} of the UCI clients have their upper bound exceeded more than twice as often as stated; with the recommended layer, {{tl_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_underprot}} and {{tl_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_underprot}}. The *average* cost changes little ({{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_cost10_delta}}, {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_cost20_delta}} and {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_cost40_delta}} on BDG2 for penalty ratios of 10, 20 and 40), and the mean cost of the worst decile of buildings changes by {{tl_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_worst10_delta}}. The layer does not make the portfolio cheaper. It redistributes the planning margin from regular buildings, which pooled calibration over-protects, to volatile buildings, which it under-protects, so that far fewer buildings systematically violate their stated reliability. Whether this has monetary value depends on whether contracts or settlement are building-specific; with a portfolio-level linear penalty, as in this cost model, it has none.
