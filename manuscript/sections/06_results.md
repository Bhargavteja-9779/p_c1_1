# Results {#sec:results}

All shares in this section use one unit, the building: a building's metrics are averaged over the seeds in which it was a target. "Under-covered" means coverage below 0.85 at the 90 % target, evaluated over the 56-day window, in the first week, or in each week of weeks 2–8. The *oracle reference* is the share that perfectly calibrated *static* intervals with the building's own day-to-day error dependence would show (Section [-@sec:setup]); it is the same for every method.

## Pooled calibration leaves many new buildings unreliable

The global LightGBM forecaster reaches a median CV(RMSE) of {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_lgb}} on new BDG2 buildings over their first 56 forecast days (seasonal naive: {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_naive}}; MLP: {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_mlp}}) and {{point_UCI_cvrmse_lgb}} on UCI clients (naive {{point_UCI_cvrmse_naive}}; MLP {{point_UCI_cvrmse_mlp}}). Its pooled conformal intervals are well calibrated on average ({{main_BDG2_CQR_mean_cov}} mean coverage on BDG2) but not building by building (Table [-@tbl:main]). Pooled CQR leaves {{tt_BDG2_CQR_under}} of the BDG2 buildings and {{tt_UCI_CQR_under}} of the UCI clients under-covered over the window, against an oracle reference of {{tt_BDG2_ref_win}} and {{tt_UCI_ref_win}}, and in an average week of weeks 2–8 it leaves {{tt_BDG2_CQR_wk_late}} and {{tt_UCI_CQR_wk_late}} under-covered (oracle {{tt_BDG2_ref_week}} and {{tt_UCI_ref_week}}). Over eight weeks, {{tt_BDG2_CQR_any80}} of the BDG2 buildings have at least one week below 0.80. Under-coverage is concentrated in hard-to-forecast buildings (Spearman correlation of coverage with CV(RMSE): {{ea_BDG2_rho_CQR}}), and it is not a borderline effect: {{th_BDG2_CQR_under_5pt}} of the BDG2 buildings miss the target by more than five points (Fig. [-@fig:cdf]). Direct probabilistic models do not solve the problem (MLP-QR: {{main_BDG2_MLPQR_under_rate}} under-covered on BDG2; the Gaussian MLP lowers this to {{main_BDG2_MLPGauss_under_rate}} only by over-covering on average at a median interval score of {{main_BDG2_MLPGauss_rel_IS}} instead of {{main_BDG2_CQR_rel_IS}}). A uniform level offset between calibration and target periods explains only {{off_BDG2_drop}} points of the pooled excess; the rest is heterogeneity between buildings.

## Week-by-week reliability with two-timescale calibration {#sec:weekly}

TTSC removes most of this unreliability at every time scale (Table [-@tbl:main], Fig. [-@fig:ttsc]). The primary evidence are the held-out runs (seeds 2–4), which played no role in its design. There, TTSC leaves {{tt_BDG2h_TTSC_wk_late}} of the BDG2 buildings and {{tt_UCIh_TTSC_wk_late}} of the UCI clients under-covered in an average week of weeks 2–8 (pooled CQR: {{tt_BDG2h_CQR_wk_late}} and {{tt_UCIh_CQR_wk_late}}; shrinkage only: {{tt_BDG2h_HSCCQR_recency_capped_wk_late}} and {{tt_UCIh_HSCCQR_recency_capped_wk_late}}), {{tt_BDG2h_TTSC_wk1}} and {{tt_UCIh_TTSC_wk1}} in the first week (pooled CQR: {{tt_BDG2h_CQR_wk1}} and {{tt_UCIh_CQR_wk1}}), and {{tt_BDG2h_TTSC_under}} and {{tt_UCIh_TTSC_under}} over the window. Over all seeds the figures are the same: {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_wk_late}} and {{tt_UCI_TTSC_wk_late}} per week in weeks 2–8, *below* the oracle reference of {{tt_BDG2_ref_week}} and {{tt_UCI_ref_week}}, and the share of buildings with any week below 0.80 falls from {{tt_BDG2_CQR_any80}} to {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_any80}} (BDG2) and from {{tt_UCI_CQR_any80}} to {{tt_UCI_TTSC_any80}} (UCI). Static calibration cannot get below the oracle reference, because even perfectly calibrated static intervals inherit the week-to-week variation of a building's errors; closed-loop calibration can, because it widens the interval in a week in which the building misses and narrows it in a week in which it over-covers.

Against the strongest alternatives the gains are significant but differ in size (Supplementary Table S23). Against shrinkage alone, TTSC lowers the weekly under-covered share in weeks 2–8 by {{tc_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_capped_wk_late_abs}} percentage points on BDG2 (95 % site-cluster bootstrap CI {{tc_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_capped_wk_late_ci}}) and {{tc_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_capped_wk_late_abs}} on UCI, and against ACI tuned, like the other single-timescale strategies, for window-level coverage by {{tc_BDG2_ACICQR_cov_wk_late_abs}} and {{tc_UCI_ACICQR_cov_wk_late_abs}}. Feedback tuned for the same weekly objective and with the same cap is a much stronger competitor. Against cumulative ACI tuned this way, TTSC still lowers weekly under-coverage by {{tc_BDG2_ACI_weeklytuned_wk_late_abs}} (CI {{tc_BDG2_ACI_weeklytuned_wk_late_ci}}; held-out {{tc_BDG2h_ACI_weeklytuned_wk_late_abs}}) and {{tc_UCI_ACI_weeklytuned_wk_late_abs}} points (held-out {{tc_UCIh_ACI_weeklytuned_wk_late_abs}}), the share of buildings with any week below 0.80 by {{tc_BDG2_ACI_weeklytuned_any80_abs}} points on BDG2, and first-week under-coverage by {{tc_BDG2_ACI_weeklytuned_wk1_abs}} points on BDG2; all weekly comparisons with shrinkage, quantile shrinkage, window-tuned ACI, pooled CQR and weekly-tuned ACI are significant after Holm adjustment on BDG2. Against 7-day windowed feedback *without* the prior (κ = ∞), weekly under-coverage is not significantly different (a reduction of {{tc_BDG2_Windowed_ACI_weeklytuned_wk_late_abs}} points on BDG2, CI {{tc_BDG2_Windowed_ACI_weeklytuned_wk_late_ci}}), but feedback without the prior leaves {{tt_BDG2_Windowed_ACI_weeklytuned_under}} and {{tt_UCI_Windowed_ACI_weeklytuned_under}} of the buildings systematically under-covered over the window, above the oracle reference, against {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_under}} and {{tt_UCI_TTSC_under}} with TTSC, and it is worse in the first week on BDG2 ({{tt_BDG2_Windowed_ACI_weeklytuned_wk1}} versus {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_wk1}}). Two conclusions follow. Most of the weekly gain comes from feedback that is tuned for weekly reliability and capped, which the usual window-level tuning misses; and the shrinkage prior is what keeps systematic (window-level) and early under-coverage low while feedback works. On BDG2, TTSC has the lowest first-week and weekly under-coverage of all strategies and window under-coverage within 0.2 points of the best; on UCI, weekly-tuned feedback without the prior is slightly better in the first week ({{tt_UCI_ACI_weeklytuned_wk1}} versus {{tt_UCI_TTSC_wk1}}).

*Cost in sharpness.* The fast component spends some width. The median relative interval score of TTSC is {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_rel_IS_median_vs_cqr}} (BDG2) and {{tt_UCI_TTSC_rel_IS_median_vs_cqr}} (UCI) above that of pooled CQR as a ratio of medians, and {{tc_BDG2_CQR_log_rel_IS}} and {{tc_UCI_CQR_log_rel_IS}} as the median of paired building-level log ratios; at the 95 % level the ratio of medians rises to about 6–7 % (Table [-@tbl:robust]). The mean interval score changes by only {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_rel_IS_mean_vs_cqr}} and {{tt_UCI_TTSC_rel_IS_mean_vs_cqr}}, because the fast component also removes some very wide intervals. TTSC therefore does not satisfy the 2 % sharpness rule used to select among single-timescale strategies (Section [-@sec:design]); it buys weekly reliability with a few percent of median sharpness. The gain $\gamma$, selected on calibration buildings, was {{tp_gamma_main}} in the main runs; it reached the edge of its grid in {{tp_gamma_edge_share}} of all runs.

*Window and cap.* The window was fixed at the settlement period of seven days. With $\gamma$ re-selected for each window, $W=3$ and $W=7$ give similar weekly under-coverage ({{tw_BDG2_W3_wk_late}} and {{tw_BDG2_W7_wk_late}} on BDG2, {{tw_UCI_W3_wk_late}} and {{tw_UCI_W7_wk_late}} on UCI), $W=7$ the fewest buildings with any week below 0.80 ({{tw_BDG2_W7_any80}} versus {{tw_BDG2_W3_any80}} on BDG2), and longer windows are clearly worse ({{tw_BDG2_W14_wk_late}} and {{tw_BDG2_W28_wk_late}} for 14 and 28 days; Supplementary Table S30). The choice of the settlement period is therefore not critical as long as the window is short. The radius is at the cap on {{tw_BDG2_W7_at_cap}} of building-days (UCI: {{tw_UCI_W7_at_cap}}).

## Why two timescales {#sec:ablation}

The ablations in Table [-@tbl:main] separate the components. *Shrinkage alone* ($\gamma=0$) provides the informed start: first-week under-coverage of {{tt_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_capped_wk1}} on BDG2 and window under-coverage of {{tt_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_capped_under}}, but {{tt_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_capped_wk_late}} per week in weeks 2–8, above the oracle reference, because a distributional estimate follows within-week changes only slowly. *Feedback alone* reaches the weekly level of TTSC only when it is windowed and tuned for weekly reliability, and then misses the window-level and early reliability that the prior provides (above). *Cumulative* instead of windowed feedback on top of shrinkage, with the same gain, keeps old disturbances in the level and is less effective in weeks 2–8 ({{tt_BDG2_TTSC_cumulative_feedback_wk_late}} versus {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_wk_late}}), although it fails slightly fewer weekly checks ({{tt_BDG2_TTSC_cumulative_feedback_breach_wk}} versus {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_breach_wk}}). Using the building's own data one day later (for gate closures before the end of the previous day) costs much of the gain ({{tt_BDG2_TTSC_delay_1d_wk_late}} in weeks 2–8, {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_delay_1d_wk1}} in week 1), so TTSC benefits from the most recent data an operator can obtain. Without the cap, the fast component produces very wide intervals (median interval score {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_uncapped_rel_IS_median}} versus {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_rel_IS_median}}); the cap is part of the method.

The slow component itself was studied in detail (Supplementary Section S5). Shrinkage of the calibration towards the building's own scores is a property of a family rather than of one estimator: quantile shrinkage performs almost as well as score shrinkage over the window and in the first week, and both remove nearly all window-level excess ({{tt_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_capped_under}} and {{tt_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_cov_under}} on BDG2). In the frontier runs, the first-week gain comes from a small pseudo-count ({{mk_BDG2_under_wk1_rho0.95_k48}} with and {{mk_BDG2_under_wk1_rho1.0_k48}} without recency at $\kappa$ = 48 h), recency acts later in the window, and similarity-localised priors, onboarding-stage matching and stage-wise studentisation do not help (Fig. [-@fig:ablation]). Over the reliability–sharpness frontier of each family (Fig. [-@fig:frontier]), ACI reaches window-level reliability only in a narrow band of step sizes and never repairs the first week without a large loss of sharpness ({{fr_BDG2_ACI_0.005_ISdelta}} median interval score at $\gamma$ = 0.005, {{fr_BDG2_ACI_0.01_ISdelta}} at 0.01); tuned for weekly reliability and capped instead, feedback performs much better (Section [-@sec:weekly]).

## Generalisation and stress tests

Table [-@tbl:robust] repeats the comparison under every other protocol. In {{to_n_ttsc_lowest_weekly}} of {{to_n_conditions}} conditions, TTSC has the lowest weekly under-covered share in weeks 2–8 among all strategies; the exceptions are three of the {{to_n_fb_conditions}} conditions in which windowed feedback without the prior was also run (nominal 95 % and 80 % on BDG2 and BDG2 → UCI), where it is lower by at most one point. TTSC has the lowest weekly share on campuses that contributed no training or calibration building ({{to_lso_BDG2_TTSC_wk_late}} versus {{to_lso_BDG2_CQR_wk_late}} for pooled CQR), in the hard transfer from Portuguese UCI clients to BDG2 ({{to_xds:UCItoBDG2_BDG2_TTSC_wk_late}} versus {{to_xds:UCItoBDG2_BDG2_CQR_wk_late}}), under meter noise, spikes, missing data, outages, level shifts, ramps, small calibration pools and a weak forecaster. Meter noise on the new buildings, which breaks pooled calibration from clean calibration buildings ({{to_noise15_BDG2_CQR_wk_late}} under-covered per week at 15 % noise), is absorbed by both timescales ({{to_noise15_BDG2_TTSC_wk_late}}). Two stresses remain hard for every strategy: a level shift in the first two weeks keeps first-week under-coverage near 40 % for all methods, and after a level increase of 40 % at day 28 TTSC only reaches the oracle reference ({{to_shiftp40_BDG2_TTSC_wk_late}} versus {{to_shiftp40_BDG2_TTSC_ref_week}}). Across conditions, the median interval score of TTSC lies between {{to_isdelta_min}} and {{to_isdelta_max}} of pooled CQR's. On a stricter weekly criterion, any week below target − 5 points, TTSC is not better than pooled CQR under several stress tests (Supplementary Table S25), because single weeks with a few extra misses remain common for every method.

## Operational case: weekly-settled capacity-headroom offers {#sec:operational}

The following case is stylised; it is not modelled on the rules of a specific market or programme, but it captures a feature common to performance-based flexibility and demand-response schemes: performance is assessed per participant and per period. Consider an aggregator that offers, for every enrolled building and hour, day-ahead capacity headroom below a contracted limit, planned on the upper interval bound, and whose performance is checked per building and week: a week fails if the realised load exceeds the planned upper bound in more than 10 % of the hours, twice the nominal 5 %. With pooled CQR, {{tt_BDG2_CQR_breach_wk}} of the building-weeks fail this check on BDG2 and {{tt_UCI_CQR_breach_wk}} on UCI. TTSC reduces this to {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_breach_wk}} and {{tt_UCI_TTSC_breach_wk}} (paired reductions of {{tc_BDG2_CQR_breach_wk_abs}} and {{tc_UCI_CQR_breach_wk_abs}} points, CIs {{tc_BDG2_CQR_breach_wk_ci}} and {{tc_UCI_CQR_breach_wk_ci}}), and below shrinkage alone ({{tt_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_capped_breach_wk}}), window-tuned ACI ({{tt_BDG2_ACICQR_cov_breach_wk}}) and weekly-tuned ACI ({{tt_BDG2_ACI_weeklytuned_breach_wk}}); the difference to weekly-tuned feedback is small and not significant after Holm adjustment. The planning cost, the planned upper bound plus a penalty proportional to violations (Eq. [-@eq:cost]), changes by {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_cost10_vs_cqr}}, {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_cost20_vs_cqr}} and {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_cost40_vs_cqr}} on BDG2 for penalty ratios of 10, 20 and 40 (UCI: {{tt_UCI_TTSC_cost10_vs_cqr}}, {{tt_UCI_TTSC_cost20_vs_cqr}}, {{tt_UCI_TTSC_cost40_vs_cqr}}). The average headroom cost is therefore essentially unchanged; TTSC moves the planning margin to the buildings and weeks that need it, so that fewer weekly performance checks fail. The value of this depends on how failed checks are penalised. Under a purely linear, portfolio-level penalty it is small; where failed weekly checks carry a fixed charge, a reputational cost or exclusion from the programme, it is the relevant improvement.

## Computational cost

The online step of TTSC (binning the new scores, updating the decayed counts and the windowed level, and reading one mixture quantile) takes well under a millisecond per building-day on one CPU core ({{time_BDG2_recency_apply_ms}} ms for the shrinkage step, against {{time_BDG2_lgb_infer_ms}} ms for the LightGBM inference itself). The state per building is one count vector of {{time_BDG2_state_KB}} kB plus seven daily miss counts; leave-one-building-out selection of both parameters takes seconds on a single core. Cost per building-day is constant in the number of buildings and in onboarding time.

## Where the calibration still fails {#sec:errors}

Even with TTSC, single weeks of under-coverage remain common: {{tt_BDG2_TTSC_any85}} of the BDG2 buildings and {{tt_UCI_TTSC_any85}} of the UCI clients have at least one week below 0.85 over eight weeks (pooled CQR: {{tt_BDG2_CQR_any85}} and {{tt_UCI_CQR_any85}}; static oracle: {{tt_BDG2_ref_any}} and {{tt_UCI_ref_any}}). Occasional weekly misses are therefore unavoidable with intervals of this sharpness, and contracts should tolerate them; what TTSC removes is their concentration in the same buildings week after week.
