# Results {#sec:results}

## Pooled calibration leaves many new buildings unreliable

The global LightGBM forecaster reaches a median CV(RMSE) of {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_lgb}} on new BDG2 buildings over their first 56 forecast days (seasonal naive: {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_naive}}; MLP: {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_mlp}}) and {{point_UCI_cvrmse_lgb}} on UCI clients (naive {{point_UCI_cvrmse_naive}}; MLP {{point_UCI_cvrmse_mlp}}). The forecasters are therefore reasonable; the question is whether their intervals can be trusted building by building.

Table [-@tbl:main] shows that they cannot when calibration is pooled. Conformalised quantile regression (CQR) attains a mean per-building coverage of {{main_BDG2_CQR_mean_cov}} on BDG2, close to the 0.90 target, yet {{main_BDG2_CQR_under_rate}} of the new buildings have coverage below 0.85, and the 10th percentile of per-building coverage is {{main_BDG2_CQR_p10_cov}}. On UCI the share is {{main_UCI_CQR_under_rate}}. Direct quantile regression with a neural network (MLP-QR: {{main_BDG2_MLPQR_under_rate}} on BDG2) does not solve the problem, and the Gaussian MLP lowers under-coverage ({{main_BDG2_MLPGauss_under_rate}}) only by over-covering on average ({{main_BDG2_MLPGauss_mean_cov}}) with a median interval score {{main_BDG2_MLPGauss_rel_IS}} instead of {{main_BDG2_CQR_rel_IS}}. If every building were perfectly calibrated, sampling noise alone would leave about {{main_BDG2_CQR_under_floor}} (BDG2) and {{main_UCI_CQR_under_floor}} (UCI) of the buildings below 0.85 over the window. Pooled calibration therefore produces more than ten times as many unreliable buildings as sampling noise would. Fig. [-@fig:cdf] shows the long lower tails of the per-building coverage distributions.

## Building-adaptive calibration strategies compared

All strategies in this subsection use the same quantile models and CQR scores; they differ only in how a new building's own scores enter the calibration. The recency-weighted shrinkage layer (HSC-CQR recency) leaves {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}} of the BDG2 buildings and {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}} of the UCI clients under-covered, which is within the sampling-noise reference of {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_under_floor}} and {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_under_floor}}. The 10th percentile of per-building coverage rises from {{main_BDG2_CQR_p10_cov}} to {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_p10_cov}} (BDG2). Its median interval score differs from pooled CQR by {{rec_HSCCQR_recency_is_ratio_BDG2}} (BDG2) and {{rec_HSCCQR_recency_is_ratio_UCI}} (UCI). Paired per building, the layer reduces the absolute coverage deviation for {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_vs_CQR_abs_dev_better}} of the BDG2 buildings (rank-biserial $r$ = {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_vs_CQR_abs_dev_r}}, Holm-adjusted $p$ {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_vs_CQR_abs_dev_p}}) and {{test_main_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_vs_CQR_abs_dev_better}} of the UCI clients, while the interval scores are statistically indistinguishable from CQR's (Holm-adjusted $p$ {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_vs_CQR_rel_IS_p}} on BDG2 and {{test_main_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_vs_CQR_rel_IS_p}} on UCI). On BDG2, every reliability comparison in this section is also significant when the ten site means are tested ($p \le$ {{max_psite_primary_BDG2}}) and when the largest site is excluded (Supplementary Table S5).

The competing mechanisms behave as their designs suggest (Table [-@tbl:main]). *Self-calibration* (Self-CQR) is reliable later in the window but leaves {{main_BDG2_SelfCQR_under_wk1}} of the BDG2 buildings under-covered in the first week, when a few dozen own scores yield noisy quantiles. *Temporal quantile adjustment* (TQA-B-CQR: {{main_BDG2_TQABCQR_under_rate}}) and *GHCP-style pooling* (GHCP-CQR: {{main_BDG2_GHCPCQR_under_rate}}) remain close to pooled CQR. TQA bounds its adjustment by design, and GHCP never gives the new building more weight than one reference building, which secures marginal validity across buildings but cannot correct a building whose errors are systematically larger than average. *Quantile shrinkage* is the closest competitor. Tuned with the same coverage objective (QShrink-CQR (cov)), it leaves {{main_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_cov_under_rate}} and {{main_UCI_QShrinkCQR_cov_under_rate}} under-covered. The proposed layer has the smaller coverage deviation for {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_vs_QShrinkCQR_cov_abs_dev_better}} of the BDG2 buildings ($p$ {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_vs_QShrinkCQR_cov_abs_dev_p}}), at a slightly higher interval score (better for {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_vs_QShrinkCQR_cov_rel_IS_better}} of the buildings). *Online level control* (ACI-CQR) tuned for the interval score selects small step sizes and leaves {{main_BDG2_ACICQR_under_rate}} of the BDG2 buildings under-covered. Tuned for coverage, it selects the largest step size of the grid ($\gamma$ = {{tuned_BDG2_aci_cqr_gamma_cov}}) and reaches nominal coverage for almost every building, but its median interval score nearly doubles ({{main_BDG2_ACICQR_cov_rel_IS}} vs {{main_BDG2_CQR_rel_IS}}). Its intervals alternate between very wide and very narrow as the level is pushed up and down. Under the pre-specified sharpness constraint (Section [-@sec:design]), this variant is not eligible.

## Onboarding dynamics and the reliability–sharpness frontier {#sec:frontier}

Fig. [-@fig:weekly] resolves reliability by week. In the first week, pooled CQR leaves {{wk_BDG2_CQR_w1}} (BDG2) and {{wk_UCI_CQR_w1}} (UCI) of the buildings under-covered. ACI-CQR does not change this ({{wk_BDG2_ACICQR_w1}} and {{wk_UCI_ACICQR_w1}}), and the shrinkage layer without recency improves it only partly ({{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_simple_cov_under_wk1}} and {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_simple_cov_under_wk1}}). The recency-weighted layer reduces these shares to {{wk_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_w1}} and {{wk_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_w1}}. From the second week on it stays close to the level expected for perfectly calibrated intervals ({{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_under_floor_week}} on BDG2 for weekly coverage, which is noisier than coverage over the window), while pooled calibration stays several points above that level in every week.

![Share of buildings whose coverage in a given week after metering started is below 0.85 (main protocol, five seeds). Dotted: share expected from sampling noise if every building were perfectly calibrated (normal approximation with each building's day-block standard error, weekly scale).](figures/Fig_weekly.png){#fig:weekly}

Fig. [-@fig:frontier] maps every strategy family over its full parameter grid on the target buildings. This is for transparency only; nothing was selected on target buildings. Over the whole window, the families reach similar endpoints (panels a, b). Shrinkage with or without recency, quantile shrinkage and ACI at a well-chosen step size ($\gamma$ = 0.002: {{fr_BDG2_ACI_0.002_under}} under-covered on BDG2) can all bring the under-covered share close to zero without loss of sharpness. Two differences remain. First, ACI attains this only in a narrow band of step sizes. One grid step further ($\gamma$ = 0.005), the median interval score rises by {{fr_BDG2_ACI_0.005_ISdelta}} on BDG2, and at $\gamma$ = 0.01 by {{fr_BDG2_ACI_0.01_ISdelta}}. The shrinkage families degrade gracefully over almost two orders of magnitude of $\kappa$. Second, and decisive for onboarding, the families differ in the first week (panels c, d). No ACI step size that preserves sharpness brings the first-week share below {{fr_BDG2_ACI_0.002_wk1}} on BDG2, whereas the recency-weighted shrinkage family reaches {{fr_BDG2_HSC_decay_0.95_48_wk1}} at $\kappa$ = 48 h. Leave-one-building-out selection chose $\kappa$ = {{tuned_BDG2_hsc_cqr_recency}} h on BDG2 and {{tuned_UCI_hsc_cqr_recency}} h on UCI.

## Which design elements matter {#sec:ablation}

Fig. [-@fig:ablation] isolates the elements of the layer. *Shrinkage* is indispensable: without it ($\kappa=\infty$), {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_shrink_under_rate}} of the BDG2 buildings remain under-covered. *The selection objective* matters next. Selected by the interval score, the pseudo-count of the layer without recency was {{tuned_BDG2_kappa_simple}} h on BDG2 (HSC-CQR simple: {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_simple_under_rate}} under-covered); selected by per-building coverage it was {{tuned_BDG2_hsc_cqr_simple_cov}} h ({{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_simple_cov_under_rate}}). The interval score is almost flat in $\kappa$ (Fig. [-@fig:frontier]), so it gives little guidance on per-building reliability. *Recency weighting* then reduces the remaining under-coverage from {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_simple_cov_under_rate}} to {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}} (BDG2) and acts above all in the first week ({{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_under_wk1}} vs {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_simple_cov_under_wk1}} on BDG2; {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_under_wk1}} vs {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_simple_cov_under_wk1}} on UCI). Compared with a fixed one-week pseudo-count without recency, the donor–local mixture of [@jeon2026] (HSC-CQR fixed-κ: {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_fixedkappa_under_rate}}), the proposed layer reduces the coverage deviation for {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_vs_HSCCQR_fixedkappa_abs_dev_better}} of the BDG2 buildings ($p$ {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_vs_HSCCQR_fixedkappa_abs_dev_p}}) at a slightly higher interval score.

Three refinements do not help. The similarity-localised population distribution and onboarding-stage matching (HSC-CQR: {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_under_rate}}) add nothing over the stage-agnostic simple variant. After normalisation by scale and conditioning of the forecaster on history-derived features, the remaining between-building differences in score distributions appear to be driven by events rather than by load shape, and the forecaster already knows how much history it has. Stage-wise studentisation alone (SSC-CQR) leaves {{main_BDG2_SSCCQR_under_rate}} of the BDG2 buildings under-covered despite its stage-wise guarantee across buildings (Proposition 1). Coverage averaged over new buildings at every day does not imply coverage for each building. Combined with shrinkage (HSC-SSC-CQR), studentisation matches the proposed layer in median terms ({{main_BDG2_HSCSSCCQR_under_rate}}), but a few buildings with a large running error scale receive extremely wide intervals. Its *mean* capacity-headroom cost is {{main_BDG2_HSCSSCCQR_cost20_delta}} above CQR's (Table [-@tbl:operational]), a tail failure that the medians hide.

## Robustness and generalisation

Table [-@tbl:robgen] stresses the strategies. Among the strategies in Table [-@tbl:robgen], the recency-weighted layer has the lowest under-covered share in every condition (with ties for self-calibration at 15 % meter noise and for quantile shrinkage in the BDG2 → UCI transfer), and its median interval score stays within ±1.5 % of pooled CQR's. Only the studentised variant HSC-SSC-CQR, which has the tail failure described above, is lower in two conditions ({{rob_calpool10_HSCSSCCQR_under_rate}} with the smallest calibration pool and {{rob_noise15_HSCSSCCQR_under_rate}} at 15 % noise). Pooled calibration from clean calibration buildings breaks down when the new buildings' meters are noisy (CQR: {{rob_noise15_CQR_under_rate}} under-covered at 15 % noise). The layer is unaffected ({{rob_noise15_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}}) because the building's own, noisy scores enter the mixture. Abrupt level changes of ±40 % at day 28 are the hardest stress for shrinkage without recency, because pre-change scores remain in the calibration. Recency weighting limits their influence ({{rob_shift+40_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}} and {{rob_shift-40_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}} under-covered, versus {{rob_shift+40_HSCCQR_simple_cov_under_rate}} and {{rob_shift-40_HSCCQR_simple_cov_under_rate}} without recency). A very small calibration pool (16 buildings) raises the share to {{rob_calpool10_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}}, and a forecaster trained on 10 % of the training buildings to {{rob_weak-forecaster_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}} (CQR: {{rob_weak-forecaster_CQR_under_rate}}).

On campuses from which no training or calibration building comes (leave-site-out), {{lso_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}} of the buildings are under-covered (CQR {{lso_BDG2_CQR_under_rate}}). With models and calibration scores from BDG2 applied to Portuguese UCI clients, the share is {{xds_B2U_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}} (CQR {{xds_B2U_CQR_under_rate}}). The opposite direction is much harder: a model and calibration population from UCI clients, without weather information, leave {{xds_U2B_CQR_under_rate}} of the BDG2 buildings under-covered with CQR and {{xds_U2B_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}} with the layer. Under such transfer the population distribution is a poor prior and the gain rests mainly on the building's own scores, but the layer still outperforms self-calibration ({{xds_U2B_SelfCQR_under_rate}}), which suffers in the first week. Results at nominal 95 % and 80 % show the same ordering, with one exception: at 80 % on BDG2, ACI-CQR leaves fewer buildings under-covered ({{main_BDG2_a20_ACICQR_under_rate}}) than the layer ({{main_BDG2_a20_HSCCQR_recency_under_rate}}) (Supplementary Tables S6–S9).

## Operational consequences and computational cost

Table [-@tbl:operational] translates the intervals into day-ahead capacity-headroom offers planned on the upper bound. With pooled CQR, {{main_BDG2_CQR_underprot}} of the BDG2 buildings and {{main_UCI_CQR_underprot}} of the UCI clients have their upper bound exceeded more than twice as often as stated. With the recency-weighted layer, these shares fall to {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_underprot}} and {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_underprot}}. The *average* cost changes little: {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_cost10_delta}}, {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_cost20_delta}} and {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_recency_cost40_delta}} on BDG2 for penalty ratios of 10, 20 and 40 (UCI: {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_cost10_delta}}, {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_cost20_delta}}, {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_recency_cost40_delta}}). The layer does not make the portfolio cheaper on average. It redistributes risk: the planning margin moves from regular buildings, which pooled calibration over-protects, to volatile buildings, which it under-protects, so that no building systematically violates its stated reliability. The higher the penalty for violations, the more this pays off. Aggressive online level control (ACI-CQR (cov)) achieves even fewer under-protected buildings, but at {{main_BDG2_ACICQR_cov_cost20_delta}} higher cost on BDG2.

Once the scores are stored, the layer adds {{time_BDG2_recency_apply_ms}} ms per building-day on one CPU core (BDG2; pooled CQR: {{time_BDG2_cqr_apply_ms}} ms). The calibration store needs {{time_BDG2_store_MB}} MB for about 170 calibration buildings, and leave-one-building-out selection of $\kappa$ takes {{time_BDG2_recency_tune_s}} s.
