# Results {#sec:results}

## Point accuracy and the reliability problem (RQ1)

The global LightGBM forecaster reaches a median CV(RMSE) of {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_lgb}} on new BDG2 buildings over their first 56 forecast days (seasonal naive: {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_naive}}; MLP: {{point_BDG2_cvrmse_mlp}}) and {{point_UCI_cvrmse_lgb}} on UCI clients (naive {{point_UCI_cvrmse_naive}}; MLP {{point_UCI_cvrmse_mlp}}). The forecasters are therefore reasonable; the question is whether their intervals can be trusted building by building.

Tables [-@tbl:mainbdg2] and [-@tbl:mainuci] show that they cannot when calibration is pooled. Stage-matched split conformal prediction (SCP-stage) attains a mean per-building coverage of {{main_BDG2_SCPstage_mean_cov}} on BDG2, close to the 0.90 target, yet {{main_BDG2_SCPstage_under_rate}} of the new buildings have coverage below 0.85 and the 10th percentile of per-building coverage is {{main_BDG2_SCPstage_p10_cov}}. Direct quantile regression under-covers on average ({{main_BDG2_QRLGBM_mean_cov}}); conformalising it (CQR) restores the mean ({{main_BDG2_CQR_mean_cov}}) but leaves {{main_BDG2_CQR_under_rate}} of the buildings under-covered. Calibrating separately per usage type (Mondrian-usage, {{main_BDG2_Mondrianusage_under_rate}}) does not help, and the deep quantile model (MLP-QR, {{main_BDG2_MLPQR_under_rate}}) does not solve the problem either; the Gaussian MLP lowers under-coverage only by over-covering on average ({{main_BDG2_MLPGauss_mean_cov}}) with wider intervals (median relative width {{main_BDG2_MLPGauss_rel_width}} vs {{main_BDG2_CQR_rel_width}} for CQR). The same pattern appears on UCI, where SCP-stage and CQR leave {{main_UCI_SCPstage_under_rate}} and {{main_UCI_CQR_under_rate}} of the clients under-covered. Fig. [-@fig:cdf] shows the full distributions: pooled methods have long lower tails of buildings with 0.70–0.85 coverage.

## Comparison of building-adaptive calibration strategies (RQ2)

All strategies in this subsection use the same LightGBM quantile models and the same CQR scores; they differ only in how a new building's own scores enter the calibration. Pseudo-count shrinkage of the score distribution (HSC-CQR) is the most reliable of the tuned strategies on both datasets: on BDG2 its MACD is {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_macd}} and {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_under_rate}} of the buildings are under-covered (CQR: {{main_BDG2_CQR_macd}}, {{main_BDG2_CQR_under_rate}}), and on UCI {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_macd}} and {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_under_rate}} (CQR: {{main_UCI_CQR_macd}}, {{main_UCI_CQR_under_rate}}). The 10th-percentile coverage rises from {{main_BDG2_CQR_p10_cov}} to {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_p10_cov}} on BDG2. The median relative interval score is essentially unchanged ({{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_rel_IS}} vs {{main_BDG2_CQR_rel_IS}} on BDG2; {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_rel_IS}} vs {{main_UCI_CQR_rel_IS}} on UCI), so the gain in reliability does not come from wider intervals. For reference, perfectly calibrated intervals with independent misses would have an expected MACD of about {{floor_BDG2_macd}} (BDG2) and {{floor_UCI_macd}} (UCI) from sampling noise alone ({{nvalid_BDG2_median}} hourly observations per building); autocorrelated misses raise this floor further.

The competing mechanisms behave as their designs suggest. Quantile-level shrinkage (QShrink-CQR, the "regularised Mondrian") is the closest competitor (MACD {{main_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_macd}} on BDG2, {{main_UCI_QShrinkCQR_macd}} on UCI; under-covered {{main_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_under_rate}} and {{main_UCI_QShrinkCQR_under_rate}}). Self-only calibration (Self-CQR) is reliable later in the window but poor in the first week (coverage {{main_BDG2_SelfCQR_cov_early}} on BDG2), when a handful of own scores yields noisy quantiles. Adaptive conformal inference on the same scores (ACI-CQR; MACD {{main_BDG2_ACICQR_macd}}, under-covered {{main_BDG2_ACICQR_under_rate}}) adapts more slowly because it reacts to the building's errors only through the miscoverage indicator with a small step size. Temporal quantile adjustment (TQA-B-CQR; {{main_BDG2_TQABCQR_macd}}, {{main_BDG2_TQABCQR_under_rate}}) and the GHCP-style pooling (GHCP-CQR; {{main_BDG2_GHCPCQR_macd}}, {{main_BDG2_GHCPCQR_under_rate}}) remain close to pooled CQR: TQA-B bounds the quantile adjustment by design, and GHCP never gives the new building more weight than one reference building, which secures marginal validity across buildings but cannot correct a building whose errors are systematically larger than average.

Table [-@tbl:tests] reports the paired per-building tests of HSC-CQR against each strategy. On BDG2, HSC-CQR has a smaller absolute coverage deviation than CQR for {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_CQR_abs_dev_better}} of the {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_CQR_abs_dev_n}} buildings ($r_{rb}$ = {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_CQR_abs_dev_r}}, Holm-adjusted $p$ {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_CQR_abs_dev_p}}); against QShrink-CQR the corresponding values are {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_QShrinkCQR_abs_dev_better}} ($r_{rb}$ = {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_QShrinkCQR_abs_dev_r}}, $p$ {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_QShrinkCQR_abs_dev_p}}), against Self-CQR {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_SelfCQR_abs_dev_better}} ($r_{rb}$ = {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_SelfCQR_abs_dev_r}}, $p$ {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_SelfCQR_abs_dev_p}}) and against ACI-CQR {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_ACICQR_abs_dev_better}} ($r_{rb}$ = {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_ACICQR_abs_dev_r}}, $p$ {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_ACICQR_abs_dev_p}}). The reliability gain does not cost sharpness: HSC-CQR's per-building interval score is lower than CQR's for {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_CQR_rel_IS_better}} of the buildings ($r_{rb}$ = {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_CQR_rel_IS_r}}, $p$ {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_CQR_rel_IS_p}}) and statistically indistinguishable from QShrink-CQR's ($p$ {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_QShrinkCQR_rel_IS_p}}). The UCI comparisons, based on {{test_main_UCI_HSCCQR_vs_CQR_abs_dev_n}} clients, are reported in the same table. Fig. [-@fig:tradeoff] summarises reliability and sharpness of all methods.

The calibration layer is not specific to LightGBM: applied to the quantiles of the MLP quantile model, it reduces the MACD from {{main_BDG2_CQR_MLP_macd}} (CQR on MLP quantiles) to {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_MLP_macd}} and the under-covered share from {{main_BDG2_CQR_MLP_under_rate}} to {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_MLP_under_rate}} on BDG2 ({{main_UCI_CQR_MLP_macd}} → {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_MLP_macd}} on UCI). On the absolute-residual score, HSC improves on pooled SCP (MACD {{main_BDG2_HSC_macd}} vs {{main_BDG2_SCPstage_macd}}) but not on self-only calibration (Self-CP: {{main_BDG2_SelfCP_macd}}); for that score the stage-agnostic simple variant is the better choice ({{main_BDG2_HSC_simple_macd}}).

## Onboarding dynamics

Fig. [-@fig:stage] resolves coverage and interval score by onboarding day. All methods have their largest interval scores in the first days, when the forecaster has least history. Pooled calibration keeps a fixed radius per stage bin and therefore cannot react to an individual building, whereas the shrinkage strategies move towards the building's own error level as scores accumulate. In the first week, HSC-CQR's mean coverage is {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_cov_early}} on BDG2 and {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_cov_early}} on UCI (CQR: {{main_BDG2_CQR_cov_early}} and {{main_UCI_CQR_cov_early}}); until own scores accumulate, every strategy can only be as reliable as its population calibration.

## Which design elements matter (RQ3)

Table [-@tbl:ablation] and Fig. [-@fig:ablation] isolate the elements of the calibration layer. Removing the shrinkage towards own scores (κ = ∞) is decisive: HSC-CQR's under-covered share rises from {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_under_rate}} to {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_shrink_under_rate}} on BDG2 and from {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_under_rate}} to {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_shrink_under_rate}} on UCI. Removing the similarity prior (K = all) or the stage matching changes the MACD by less than its seed-to-seed standard deviation (BDG2: {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_kNN_macd}} and {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_stage_macd}} vs {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_macd}}); the simple variant without both refinements reaches {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_simple_macd}} on BDG2 and {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_simple_macd}} on UCI. The value of the pseudo-count matters more than the refinements. Fixing it at one week of hourly scores (κ = 168 h, a donor–local mixture with fixed pseudo-count) gives a MACD of {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_fixedkappa_macd}} and {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_fixedkappa_under_rate}} under-covered buildings on BDG2 at a median interval score of {{main_BDG2_HSCCQR_fixedkappa_rel_IS}}, i.e. more reliable but marginally less sharp than the tuned HSC-CQR (paired tests: $r_{rb}$ = {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_HSCCQR_fixedkappa_abs_dev_r}} for the coverage deviation and {{test_main_BDG2_HSCCQR_vs_HSCCQR_fixedkappa_rel_IS_r}} for the interval score, positive values favouring the tuned variant); on UCI the two are statistically indistinguishable ({{main_UCI_HSCCQR_fixedkappa_macd}} vs {{main_UCI_HSCCQR_macd}}; $p$ {{test_main_UCI_HSCCQR_vs_HSCCQR_fixedkappa_abs_dev_p}}). Leave-one-building-out tuning on the interval score selected κ values of {{tuned_BDG2_kappa_simple}} h for the simple variant on BDG2 and {{tuned_UCI_kappa_simple}} h on UCI (across seeds); the tuning objective rewards sharpness as well as coverage (see the sensitivity analysis below).

## Robustness (RQ4)

Table [-@tbl:robust] and Fig. [-@fig:robust] stress the strategies on BDG2. With 15 % meter noise on the new buildings, pooled calibration from clean calibration buildings breaks down (SCP-stage: {{rob_noise15_SCPstage_under_rate}} under-covered; CQR: {{rob_noise15_CQR_under_rate}}), whereas HSC-CQR leaves {{rob_noise15_HSCCQR_under_rate}} under-covered because the building's own, noisy scores enter the mixture. Missing data (up to 40 % of hours) mainly delays adaptation (HSC-CQR {{rob_missing40_HSCCQR_under_rate}} vs CQR {{rob_missing40_CQR_under_rate}}). Abrupt level changes of ±40 % at day 28 degrade every strategy, because pre-change scores remain in the calibration, but the shrinkage strategies least: with the downward shift HSC-CQR leaves {{rob_shift-40_HSCCQR_under_rate}} of the buildings under-covered (QShrink-CQR {{rob_shift-40_QShrinkCQR_under_rate}}, ACI-CQR {{rob_shift-40_ACICQR_under_rate}}, CQR {{rob_shift-40_CQR_under_rate}}) and with the upward shift {{rob_shift+40_HSCCQR_under_rate}} (ACI-CQR {{rob_shift+40_ACICQR_under_rate}}, CQR {{rob_shift+40_CQR_under_rate}}). A very small calibration pool (10 % of the calibration buildings, 16 buildings) raises HSC-CQR's under-covered share to {{rob_calpool10_HSCCQR_under_rate}} (CQR {{rob_calpool10_CQR_under_rate}}), whereas pools of 25 % and 50 % have little effect ({{rob_calpool25_HSCCQR_under_rate}} and {{rob_calpool50_HSCCQR_under_rate}}); a forecaster trained on only 10 % of the training buildings increases it to {{rob_weak-forecaster_HSCCQR_under_rate}} (CQR {{rob_weak-forecaster_CQR_under_rate}}).

## Generalisation (RQ5)

Under the leave-site-out protocol the target buildings are on campuses from which no training or calibration building comes. HSC-CQR's MACD is {{lso_BDG2_HSCCQR_macd}} (CQR {{lso_BDG2_CQR_macd}}; QShrink-CQR {{lso_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_macd}}), with {{lso_BDG2_HSCCQR_under_rate}} versus {{lso_BDG2_CQR_under_rate}} of the buildings under-covered (QShrink-CQR {{lso_BDG2_QShrinkCQR_under_rate}}; HSC-CQR simple {{lso_BDG2_HSCCQR_simple_under_rate}}) (Table [-@tbl:gen], Fig. [-@fig:gen]). With models and calibration scores from BDG2 applied to Portuguese UCI clients, CQR leaves {{xds_B2U_CQR_under_rate}} under-covered and HSC-CQR {{xds_B2U_HSCCQR_under_rate}}. The opposite direction is much harder: a model and calibration population from UCI clients, without weather information, leave {{xds_U2B_CQR_under_rate}} of BDG2 buildings under-covered with CQR; HSC-CQR reduces this to {{xds_U2B_HSCCQR_under_rate}}, but self-only calibration does as well ({{xds_U2B_SelfCQR_under_rate}}), because a prior from a different population adds nothing — the gain under such transfer rests entirely on the building's own scores.

## Sensitivity and computational cost

Fig. [-@fig:sens] shows the target-building MACD and interval score of HSC-CQR over the full grid of κ and K (for transparency only; not used for any choice). MACD is lowest for κ of about four days to one week of hourly scores (96–168 h; BDG2 {{sens_BDG2_macd_min}}, UCI {{sens_UCI_macd_min}}), increases moderately up to two weeks and sharply towards κ = ∞ ({{sens_BDG2_macd_inf}} on BDG2), whereas the interval score is lowest for two to four weeks (336–672 h); K has little influence. This explains why the leave-one-building-out selection, which minimises the interval score, mostly chose 336 h. Results at nominal 95 % and 80 % show the same ordering of the absolute-score strategies (supplementary Tables S3–S6).

Table [-@tbl:cost] reports costs on a 4-core CPU. Once the scores are stored, the calibration layer adds {{cost_BDG2_apply_ms}} ms per building-day and needs {{cost_BDG2_store_MB}} MB for about 170 calibration buildings; the one-off leave-one-building-out tuning takes {{cost_BDG2_tune_s}} s. Both are negligible next to training the global forecaster ({{cost_BDG2_lgb_s}} s for the three LightGBM models).
