# Experimental setup {#sec:setup}

## Data

**BDG2.** The Building Data Genome Project 2 [@miller2020bdg2] provides two years (2016–2017) of hourly meter data from non-residential buildings on university and public campuses in North America and Europe, together with site weather and building metadata. It is the dataset of the ASHRAE Great Energy Predictor III competition [@miller2020gepiii]. We use the cleaned electricity meters, site air temperature and the primary-space-usage attribute.

**UCI.** The ElectricityLoadDiagrams20112014 dataset [@trindade2015] contains 15-min consumption of {{n_uci_raw}} Portuguese electricity clients (2011–2014). We aggregate it to hourly energy and use 2012–2014, because most clients have no data in 2011. It has no weather or metadata and represents a different country, customer mix and metering infrastructure. We use it to test generality and transfer.

**Quality rules** (applied identically to all methods): runs of at least 24 identical consecutive readings (meter outage or stuck meter) are treated as missing. No value is imputed: missing hours stay missing, the features are computed from the available hours, and missing hours are excluded from every metric, so no future reading can enter a feature or a ground truth. A building is *eligible* if, in the training/calibration period only (BDG2: 2016; UCI: 2012–2013), at most 5 % of its hours are missing in gaps longer than 6 h and its mean load is at least 1 kWh/h. The target period is not used for selection: a target building only needs an onboarding window with at least 80 % valid hours, an operational rule that a monitoring platform can apply; buildings without such a window cannot be onboarded and are dropped (on average {{n_tgt_dropped_bdg2}} BDG2 and {{n_tgt_dropped_uci}} UCI targets per seed). For UCI, the two daylight-saving transition hours per year (zero or doubled readings) are missing. This leaves **{{n_bdg2}} BDG2 buildings at {{n_sites}} sites** and **{{n_uci}} UCI clients** (Fig. [-@fig:data]). Training rows whose normalised target exceeds 20 (meter restarts after near-zero history) are excluded from model fitting only. The stuck-meter rule was introduced after a smoke test showed flat-lined meters at one site; the first submission additionally required data quality in the target year, which selected targets on future information and was removed in revision (Section [-@sec:prereg]).

## Protocols and leakage control

*Random-building protocol (main).* For each of five pre-specified seeds, buildings are randomly partitioned into training (50 %, of which one tenth is held out as building-disjoint validation data for early stopping), calibration (20 %) and target (30 %) sets. The forecaster and all calibration scores use only the training period (BDG2: 2016; UCI: 2012–2013). Each calibration building is "onboarded" at a random date in the training period. Each target building is onboarded at a random date in the later period (BDG2: 2017; UCI: 2014), drawn among windows with at least 80 % valid hours. Every building is followed for 56 day-ahead forecasts ($h=1,\dots,56$). Features at stage $h$ use only the $h$ days after $t_{0,b}$ (verified by unit tests that perturb all data outside this window). Target buildings, their period and their data are therefore never seen by the forecaster, the calibration scores, or any hyper-parameter selection.

*Leave-site-out (BDG2).* The {{n_sites}} sites are grouped into five folds of similar size. All buildings of a fold are targets; the remaining buildings are split 70/30 into training and calibration sets. This tests transfer to unseen campuses, climates and operators.

*Cross-dataset.* Models and calibration scores from one dataset are applied to the target buildings of the other (BDG2→UCI and UCI→BDG2), without weather features, which UCI lacks. A BDG2→BDG2 run without weather serves as the within-dataset reference (three seeds each).

## Compared methods

All conformal-type methods share the same forecaster, scores, grid and stage bins; they differ only in how the radius $q_{b,h}$ is obtained.

* **Naive+SCP** (classical): seasonal-naive forecast (same hour one week earlier if $h\ge7$, else the previous day) with stage-matched split conformal intervals.
* **Raw-SCP**: split conformal prediction on unnormalised absolute residuals (kWh).
* **SCP**: split conformal on normalised absolute scores pooled over all stages [@lei2018].
* **SCP-stage**: as SCP, with stage-bin matching.
* **Mondrian-usage** (BDG2): SCP-stage computed separately per primary-usage class (classes with at least 10 calibration buildings), a group-conditional (Mondrian) variant [@vovk2005].
* **Self-CP**: split conformal on the building's own past scores ($\kappa=0$), falling back to SCP-stage until enough own scores exist.
* **ACI** [@gibbs2021]: adaptive conformal inference on top of the SCP-stage distribution, $\alpha_{h+1}=\alpha_h+\gamma\sum_j(\alpha-\mathrm{err}_{h,j})$, with $\gamma$ chosen by leave-one-building-out on calibration buildings from $\{0,10^{-4},2\!\times\!10^{-4},5\!\times\!10^{-4},10^{-3},2\!\times\!10^{-3},5\!\times\!10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$ (the three smallest values were added after a code audit showed that the pre-registered grid's lower edge was selected in most runs; $\gamma=0$ recovers the pooled method). The recursion keeps the unclipped $\alpha_h$, but the quantile is queried at $1-\min\{\max(\alpha_h,\alpha/10),1\}$, so a negative $\alpha_h$ gives a finite, conservative radius instead of an unbounded one (in the first submission it gave the grid maximum, which removed moderate step sizes from tuning); $\alpha_h\ge1$ gives the smallest observed score (empty-set convention).
* **QR-LGBM**: direct LightGBM quantile regression [@ke2017; @koenker1978].
* **CQR** [@romano2019]: QR-LGBM conformalised with stage-matched pooled CQR scores.
* **MLP-Gauss**: a global multilayer perceptron (256-256-128, ReLU) with a Gaussian output trained by negative log-likelihood, a deep probabilistic baseline in the spirit of DeepAR's likelihood training [@salinas2020].
* **MLP-QR**: the same network trained with the pinball loss for the two quantiles [@wang2019pinball].
* **Self-CQR** and **ACI-CQR**: self-only calibration and ACI applied to the CQR scores (same quantile models as CQR).
* **Self-CQR-fb** (switching rule): pooled CQR until the building has at least $m$ own scores, then self-only CQR; $m\in\{72,168,336\}$ h by leave-one-building-out. It answers whether simply switching to the building's own errors after a few days suffices.
* **Mondrian-CQR** (BDG2): stage-matched pooled CQR computed per primary-usage class (classes with at least 10 calibration buildings).
* **TQA-B-CQR** [@lin2022tqa]: temporal quantile adjustment ("budgeting" variant) on the CQR scores. The building's exponentially decayed mean own score (decay $\beta$ per day, normalised by the weight sum, days before the forecast only) is ranked among the calibration buildings' decayed means at the same stage; the rank $r=|\{j:\bar e_j<\bar e_b\}|/(N+1)$ is mapped to the queried level $1-a$ with $a=\alpha-\lambda g^B(r)$, $g^B(r)=C(r-(1-\alpha))$ for $r<1-\alpha$ and $r-(1-\alpha)$ otherwise, $C=\alpha^2/(1-\alpha)^2$, $\lambda\le1$ chosen per stage from the calibration buildings' ranks so that $a\ge0.01$, and the quantile is taken over the pooled scores together with a point at $+\infty$, as in the original method; $\beta\in\{0.5,0.8,0.95\}$ is selected by leave-one-building-out. The TQA-E variant is an ACI-type update and is represented by ACI-CQR.
* **GHCP-CQR**: the calibration step of generalised hierarchical conformal prediction [@mallick2026], adapted to our setting: with $M$ calibration buildings, every calibration building receives total weight $1/(M+1)$ spread over its scores (pooled over all onboarding stages), and the new building receives the same group share, spread over its observed own scores and a point mass at $+\infty$ for its not-yet-observed scores (surrogate group size selected by leave-one-building-out from $\{24,168,672\}$ h). No observations are set aside to fit a local predictor, because our global forecaster already conditions on the building's own history (this most likely favours GHCP-CQR). GHCP's finite-sample guarantee relies on within-group exchangeability, which hourly loads violate.
* **QShrink-CQR** (regularised Mondrian [@rafe2026]): quantile-level shrinkage $q=w\,q^{\mathrm{own}}+(1-w)\,q^{\mathrm{pool}}$ with $w=n_{b,h}/(n_{b,h}+\lambda)$, $q^{\mathrm{own}}$ the split-conformal quantile of the own scores ($w=0$ while undefined) and $q^{\mathrm{pool}}$ the CQR radius; $\lambda$ is selected by leave-one-building-out from the same grid and with the same objective as $\kappa$; **QShrink-CQR (λ=168)** fixes $\lambda$ at one week, matching the fixed-$\kappa$ variant. (The original method uses a fixed $\lambda$.)
* **SSC-CQR** (primary): Eq. ([-@eq:hsc]) on the CQR scores, $\kappa$ by LOBO on MACD; **SSC-CQR (κ=168)**: fixed one-week pseudo-count, i.e. a donor–local mixture with fixed pseudo-count as in [@jeon2026]; **SSC-CQR (IS-tuned)**: $\kappa$ by LOBO on the interval score; **SSC-CQR +stage+kNN**: the full pre-registered design with both refinements ($\kappa$, $K$ by LOBO on the interval score), with ablations removing each element. **SSC** variants on the absolute score are defined analogously; the pre-registered primary method was SSC +stage+kNN on the absolute score (Section [-@sec:prereg]).
* **CQR (MLP)** and **SSC-CQR (MLP)**: CQR and SSC-CQR on the quantiles of the MLP quantile model, to check that the calibration layer is not specific to LightGBM.

All leave-one-building-out selections use the same objective as for $\kappa$ (per-building MACD with interval-score tie-break), except the pre-registered SSC +stage+kNN variants and their ablations, which keep the pre-registered interval-score objective.

The neural networks use the same features (missing values set to zero with missingness indicators, standardised), Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch 4096), at most 12 epochs, and early stopping on the validation buildings (patience 3). LightGBM uses 63 leaves, learning rate 0.05, at least 200 samples per leaf, 0.8 feature and bagging fractions, L2 regularisation 1, and early stopping (50 rounds) on the validation buildings. These are conventional settings, applied identically to point and quantile models. No method was tuned on target buildings.

## Metrics and statistical analysis

For target building $b$ with valid hourly observations over its 56-day window, we compute coverage $\mathrm{cov}_b$, relative width $\overline{U-L}/\bar y_b$, and the relative interval (Winkler) score [@winkler1972; @gneiting2007]

$$
\mathrm{IS}_b=\frac{1}{\bar y_b}\,\overline{(U-L)+\tfrac{2}{\alpha}(L-y)_+ +\tfrac{2}{\alpha}(y-U)_+},
$$ {#eq:is}

a proper score that rewards narrow intervals and penalises misses in proportion to their size. Across buildings we report the mean coverage; the **mean absolute coverage deviation** $\mathrm{MACD}=\mathrm{mean}_b|\mathrm{cov}_b-(1-\alpha)|$; the **under-covered share**, i.e. the share of buildings with $\mathrm{cov}_b<1-\alpha-0.05$; the 10th percentile of $\mathrm{cov}_b$; and the medians of relative width and $\mathrm{IS}_b$. The medians are used because a few buildings with erratic loads dominate means. Means are reported in the supplementary tables. Point accuracy is reported as CV(RMSE) [@ashrae14].

We additionally report these metrics separately for onboarding week 1 ($h\le7$), week 2 and weeks 3–8. With 168 hourly observations and independent misses, a perfectly calibrated building would fall below $0.85$ coverage with probability {{iid_floor}}; autocorrelation makes this floor higher, so week-1 differences of a few percentage points in the under-covered share are within sampling noise.

The unit of statistical analysis is the target building. A building's metrics are averaged over the seeds in which it was a target. The primary method is compared with each baseline on paired per-building values of $\mathrm{IS}_b$, $|\mathrm{cov}_b-(1-\alpha)|$ and the under-coverage indicator. Because buildings at a BDG2 site share weather and operation, the **primary inference is cluster-aware**: an exact sign-flip permutation test on the site means of the paired differences (all $2^{G}$ sign patterns for $G$ sites). Two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests [@wilcoxon1945] at building level are reported as secondary; they assume independent buildings. For UCI (one site), clients are treated as independent units. The **confirmatory family**, declared before the revised runs, is the primary method versus CQR, Self-CQR-fb, ACI-CQR, TQA-B-CQR, GHCP-CQR and QShrink-CQR in the random-building protocol of each dataset; Holm's correction [@holm1979] is applied within this family for each metric. All other comparisons (variants, ablations, transfer and stress conditions) are exploratory and Holm-adjusted within their own family only. Effect sizes are the median paired difference with a 95 % site-cluster bootstrap interval (2000 resamples; over buildings for UCI) and matched-pairs rank-biserial correlations [@kerby2014], positive when the primary method is better. Seed-level results are reported as mean ± standard deviation over seeds.

## Pre-registration and deviations {#sec:prereg}

Data, protocols, seeds, forecaster settings, metrics and the original primary hypothesis — that stage-matched, similarity-localised shrinkage on the absolute score (here: SSC +stage+kNN) improves MACD and interval score over pooled calibration — were fixed in a pre-registration file (experiments/PREREGISTRATION.md) before the first full experiment. Table [-@tbl:deviations] lists every deviation, when it was made and whether target results had been seen. In particular, the primary method changed twice after target results had been seen: to the CQR score in the first submission and to the one-parameter layer with a reliability objective in this revision. The comparisons in this paper are therefore confirmatory only in the restricted sense defined above; the pre-registered hypothesis is reported separately (Section [-@sec:results]).
