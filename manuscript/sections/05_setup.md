# Experimental setup {#sec:setup}

## Data

**BDG2.** The Building Data Genome Project 2 [@miller2020bdg2] provides hourly meter data for 2016–2017 from non-residential buildings on university and public campuses in North America and Europe, with site weather and metadata; it is the dataset of the ASHRAE Great Energy Predictor III competition [@miller2020gepiii]. We use the cleaned electricity meters, site air temperature (whose timestamps are in local time: the mean diurnal temperature peaks at 14–16 h at every site) and the primary-space-usage attribute. **UCI.** ElectricityLoadDiagrams20112014 [@trindade2015] contains 15-min consumption of {{n_uci_raw}} Portuguese electricity clients; we aggregate to hourly energy, use 2012–2014 and mask the documented daylight-saving artefacts (zero hour in March, double hour in October). It has no weather or metadata and represents a different country, customer mix and metering infrastructure. Both datasets are CC-BY-4.0.

**Quality rules** (identical for all methods): runs of at least 24 identical readings (outage or stuck meter) are treated as missing; gaps of at most 6 h are interpolated for use as forecast *inputs* only — interpolated and masked hours never enter a nonconformity score or an evaluation; a building is retained if at most 5 % of its hours are missing in every period used and its mean load is at least 1 kWh/h. Of {{flow_bdg2_raw}} BDG2 electricity meters at {{flow_bdg2_sites_raw}} sites, {{n_bdg2}} buildings at {{n_sites}} sites remain (the missing-data rule removes {{flow_bdg2_removed_missing}}, the load floor {{flow_bdg2_removed_floor}}); of {{n_uci_raw}} UCI clients, {{n_uci}} remain. The missing-data rule also uses the target year, so very poorly metered buildings are excluded from the targets; Section [-@sec:limits] discusses this. Training rows whose normalised target exceeds 20 (meter restarts after near-zero history) are excluded from model fitting only.

## Protocols

*Random-building protocol (main).* For each of five seeds, buildings are partitioned into training (50 %, one tenth of which is held out for early stopping), calibration (20 %) and target (30 %) sets. The forecaster and all calibration scores use only the training period (BDG2: 2016; UCI: 2012–2013); each calibration building is onboarded at a random date in it. Each target building is onboarded at a random date in the later period (BDG2: 2017; UCI: 2014) among windows with at least 80 % valid hours, and followed for 56 day-ahead forecasts. Features at stage $h$ use only the $h$ days since onboarding. Target buildings never influence the forecaster, the calibration scores or any tuning.

*Leave-one-site-out (BDG2).* Each of the {{n_sites}} sites is held out in turn as the target set; the remaining buildings are split 70/30 into training and calibration sets, with three training seeds per site (30 trained forecasters). *Cross-dataset.* Forecaster and calibration scores from one dataset are applied to the targets of the other (BDG2→UCI, UCI→BDG2) without weather features; a BDG2→BDG2 run without weather is the reference (three seeds each). *Disjoint calibration period.* To check that calibration scores from the training period do not favour building-specific methods, the forecaster is trained on the first half of 2016 and calibration buildings are onboarded in the second half (BDG2, three seeds).

*Stress tests (BDG2, three seeds)* perturb the target buildings' onboarding windows: Gaussian meter noise of 15 % of the window mean added to history and truth, or to the history only (clean truth); 40 % of hours missing at random; a contiguous 3- or 7-day outage starting on day 3–20; a +40 % level shift from day 7 or day 28 and a −40 % shift from day 28; and random day-to-day load factors $1+\mathcal N(0,0.25^2)$ from day 28 (higher volatility than in the history). A weak forecaster is trained on 10 % of the training buildings. *Small calibration pools*: 5, 10, 20 or 40 randomly drawn calibration buildings, five draws per seed. *Other nominal levels*: all strategies are also run at 80 % and 95 %; the quantile models stay at 5 %/95 %, which the conformal radius corrects.

## Compared calibration strategies

All strategies use the same forecaster, CQR scores and score grid (details and equations in Supplementary Section S2). **CQR** [@romano2019]: pooled, stage-matched split conformal radius. **Self-CQR**: split conformal on the building's own past scores, with the pooled radius as fallback until an own quantile exists. **ACI-CQR** [@gibbs2021]: adaptive conformal inference on top of the pooled distribution with daily batches of 24 feedback signals; the step size $\gamma\in\{0,10^{-4},\dots,10^{-2}\}$ is selected by LOBO. When $\alpha_h$ falls to $1/(N+1)$ or below, the radius is the largest observed calibration score. **TQA-B-CQR** [@lin2022tqa]: temporal quantile adjustment ("budgeting" variant), ranking the building's exponentially decayed mean own score among the calibration buildings at the same stage. **GHCP-CQR**: the calibration step of generalised hierarchical conformal prediction [@mallick2026], giving every calibration building and the new building one group share. **QShrink-CQR**: quantile-level shrinkage ("regularised Mondrian" [@rafe2026]), $q=w\,q^{\mathrm{own}}+(1-w)\,q^{\mathrm{pool}}$ with $w=n/(n+\lambda)$ and $\lambda$ from the same grid as $\kappa$. **PCS-CQR**: Eq. ([-@eq:pcs]). For ACI, QShrink and PCS, both the pre-registered interval-score rule and the reliability-aware rule are reported (labelled "(MACD)" for ACI and QShrink); in addition, **ACI-CQR (coverage only)** selects $\gamma$ by LOBO coverage deviation alone, as a reference for what unconstrained coverage-targeting costs. Fixed-pseudo-count versions (**PCS-CQR κ=168**, **QShrink-CQR λ=168**, i.e. one week of hourly scores) separate the mechanism from its tuning. **HSC-CQR** is the pre-registered configuration (stage-matched, similarity-localised prior, interval-score tuning).

Reference methods without building adaptation are direct LightGBM quantile regression (**QR-LGBM**), a multilayer perceptron with Gaussian output (**MLP-Gauss**) or pinball loss (**MLP-QR**) [@wang2019pinball], and, on the absolute score, pooled (**SCP**, **SCP-stage**), Mondrian-by-usage, self-only (**Self-CP**), **ACI**, a seasonal-naive forecast with conformal intervals and the absolute-score versions **PCS** and **HSC**. **PCS-CQR (MLP)** applies the layer to the MLP quantile model. Hyper-parameters of the forecasters are conventional and identical for all methods (Supplementary Section S2); no method is tuned on target buildings.

## Metrics and statistical analysis

For target building $b$ with $n_b$ observed hours in its 56-day window we compute coverage $\mathrm{cov}_b$, relative width $\overline{U-L}/\bar y_b$ and the relative interval (Winkler) score [@winkler1972; @gneiting2007]

$$
\mathrm{IS}_b=\frac{1}{\bar y_b}\,\overline{(U-L)+\tfrac{2}{\alpha}(L-y)_+ +\tfrac{2}{\alpha}(y-U)_+}.
$$ {#eq:is}

Across buildings we report the **mean absolute coverage deviation** $\mathrm{MACD}=\mathrm{mean}_b|\mathrm{cov}_b-(1-\alpha)|$, the **under-covered share** (buildings with $\mathrm{cov}_b<1-\alpha-0.05$), the 10th percentile of $\mathrm{cov}_b$, the same quantities within onboarding weeks 1, 2, 3–4 and 5–8, and medians of width and $\mathrm{IS}_b$. Two operational measures translate the upper bound into a capacity reservation (e.g. a demand-response baseline or a day-ahead flexibility commitment sized by $U$): the share of **booking failures** — buildings whose load exceeds $U$ in more than $\alpha$ of hours, twice the nominal $\alpha/2$, which is what contractual performance factors typically penalise — and the **reserve cost**, the pinball loss of $U$ at level $1-\alpha/2$ relative to mean load (booked-but-unused capacity costs $\alpha/2$ per kWh, shortfall $1-\alpha/2$). Even a perfectly calibrated building has sampling noise: with $n_b\approx1300$ hours and independent misses, its expected $|\mathrm{cov}_b-0.9|$ is {{floor_BDG2_macd}}, and positive autocorrelation of misses makes it larger.

The unit of analysis is the target building; its metrics are averaged over the seeds in which it was a target. For each comparison we report the building-weighted mean paired difference with a 95 % percentile bootstrap interval (2000 resamples), the median difference with its bootstrap interval, the share of buildings that are better and the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation. Because BDG2 buildings are clustered in {{n_sites}} sites of very unequal size, BDG2 resamples whole sites, and the primary *p*-value is an exact sign-flip randomisation test in which whole sites change sign (1024 patterns, so $p\ge0.002$). UCI resamples and flips individual clients. *p*-values are Holm-adjusted [@holm1979] within each family (one proposed method, protocol, condition, nominal level and metric); building-level Wilcoxon tests are reported in the supplement. Metrics tested are $|\mathrm{cov}_b-(1-\alpha)|$ (CD), the under-covered indicator, $\mathrm{IS}_b$ and the booking-failure indicator. Seed-level summaries are mean ± standard deviation over seeds.

**Analysis plan and deviations.** The forecaster, protocols, metrics and the pre-registered HSC/HSC-CQR configurations were fixed in an internal analysis plan before the full experiments (experiments/PREREGISTRATION.md, not externally registered). Supplementary Table S1 lists every later change with its date, its reason and whether target results had been seen. These are: the GHCP, TQA-B and QShrink baselines (added after a prior-art audit); the PCS variants, fixed-pseudo-count versions and the reliability-aware rule (added after interim and review results showed that the planned interval-score tuning does not target reliability); the extended ACI grid; and the protocols added in revision (leave-one-site-out with three seeds per site, disjoint calibration period, further stress tests, small pools, other nominal levels). Results for PCS-CQR are therefore exploratory, and the planned primary comparison (HSC against the absolute-score baselines) is reported in full in Supplementary Table S5.

**Research software.** All code was written for this study in Python (LightGBM, PyTorch [@paszke2019], NumPy, pandas, SciPy) with the assistance of an AI coding assistant (Claude, Anthropic), which also executed the experiments and ran independent code and manuscript audits. Correctness was checked by unit tests of leakage guards and calibration primitives (Supplementary Section S2.3), by two independent code audits whose findings and fixes are logged in the repository, and by regenerating every reported number, table and figure from the released result files.
