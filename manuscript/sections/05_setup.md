# Data and experimental design {#sec:setup}

## Data

**BDG2.** The Building Data Genome Project 2 [@miller2020bdg2] provides two years (2016–2017) of hourly meter data from non-residential buildings on university and public campuses in North America and Europe, together with site weather and building metadata. It is the dataset of the ASHRAE Great Energy Predictor III competition [@miller2020gepiii]. We use the cleaned electricity meters, site air temperature and the primary-space-usage attribute.

**UCI.** The ElectricityLoadDiagrams20112014 dataset [@trindade2015] contains 15-min consumption of {{n_uci_raw}} Portuguese electricity clients (2011–2014). We aggregate it to hourly energy and use 2012–2014, because most clients have no data in 2011. It has no weather or metadata and represents a different country, customer mix and metering infrastructure. We use it to test generality and transfer.

**Quality rules** (applied identically to all methods): runs of at least 24 identical consecutive readings (meter outage or stuck meter) are treated as missing, and no load value is imputed, so missing hours are excluded from features and metrics alike. On UCI, the two daylight-saving transition hours per year are masked. Eligibility is decided on the training and calibration period only, with no look-ahead: a building is retained if at most 5 % of its hours in that period are missing outside short gaps of at most 6 h and its mean load is at least 1 kWh/h. This leaves **{{n_bdg2}} BDG2 buildings at {{n_sites}} sites** and **{{n_uci}} UCI clients** (Fig. [-@fig:data]). A target building enters the evaluation if it has an onboarding window with at least 80 % valid hours; the few that do not are dropped and counted. Air temperature gaps of at most 6 h are interpolated. Training rows whose normalised target exceeds 20 (meter restarts after near-zero history) are excluded from model fitting only.

## Protocols and leakage control

*Random-building protocol (main).* For each of five pre-specified seeds, buildings are randomly partitioned into training (50 %, of which one tenth is held out as building-disjoint validation data for early stopping), calibration (20 %) and target (30 %) sets. The forecaster and all calibration scores use only the training period (BDG2: 2016; UCI: 2012–2013). Each calibration building is "onboarded" at a random date in the training period. Each target building is onboarded at a random date in the later period (BDG2: 2017; UCI: 2014), drawn among windows with at least 80 % valid hours. Every building is followed for 56 day-ahead forecasts ($h=1,\dots,56$). Features at stage $h$ use only the $h$ days after $t_{0,b}$ (verified by unit tests that perturb all data outside this window). Target buildings, their period and their data are therefore never seen by the forecaster, the calibration scores, or any hyper-parameter selection.

*Leave-site-out (BDG2).* The {{n_sites}} sites are grouped into five folds of similar size. All buildings of a fold are targets; the remaining buildings are split 70/30 into training and calibration sets. This tests transfer to unseen campuses, climates and operators.

*Cross-dataset.* Models and calibration scores from one dataset are applied to the target buildings of the other (BDG2→UCI and UCI→BDG2), without weather features, which UCI lacks. A BDG2→BDG2 run without weather serves as the within-dataset reference (three seeds each).

*Stress tests (BDG2, three seeds).* Additive Gaussian meter noise with a standard deviation of 5 % or 15 % of the building's mean load (clipped at zero), random missing hours (20 % and 40 %) abrupt level shifts of +40 % and −40 % at day 28, a +40 % level shift at a random day between days 3 and 14 (commissioning-type change), a gradual multiplicative ramp to +40 % over the window, two outages of one to three whole days, and spikes (2 % of hours multiplied by three) are applied to the new buildings' onboarding windows (history and truth). Calibration pools are reduced to 10 %, 25 % and 50 % of the calibration buildings, and a weak forecaster is trained on 10 % of the training buildings.

## Compared methods

All conformal-type strategies use the same LightGBM quantile models, the same CQR scores (Eq. [-@eq:scores]), the same score grid and the same calibration buildings; they differ only in how the radius $q_{b,h}$ is obtained. Every tuned parameter is selected by leave-one-building-out on calibration buildings, and the parameter grids are equally fine. Implementation details and deviations from the original methods are given in Supplementary Section S3.

* *Pooled:* **CQR** [@romano2019] (stage-matched pooled CQR scores); **SCP** and **SCP-stage** (split conformal on normalised absolute residuals [@lei2018], without and with stage matching); **Raw-SCP** (unnormalised residuals); **Mondrian-usage** (BDG2; per primary-usage class [@vovk2005]); and **Naive+SCP** (seasonal-naive forecast).
* *Self-calibration:* **Self-CQR**, split conformal on the building's own past scores, falling back to pooled CQR until an own quantile exists.
* *Online level control:* **ACI-CQR** [@gibbs2021], $\alpha_{h+1}=\alpha_h+\gamma\sum_j(\alpha-\mathrm{err}_{h,j})$ on the pooled CQR distribution, with $\gamma\in\{0,10^{-4},2\cdot10^{-4},5\cdot10^{-4},10^{-3},2\cdot10^{-3},5\cdot10^{-3},10^{-2},2\cdot10^{-2},5\cdot10^{-2}\}$; a level $\alpha_h\le0$ is mapped to the largest observed calibration score.
* *Temporal and hierarchical pooling:* **TQA-B-CQR** [@lin2022tqa] (budgeted temporal quantile adjustment; the budget scale is set from calibration buildings) and **GHCP-CQR** [@mallick2026] (the new building receives the weight of one calibration building).
* *Shrinkage:* **QShrink-CQR**, quantile-level shrinkage $q=w\,q^{\mathrm{own}}+(1-w)\,q^{\mathrm{pool}}$ with $w=n_{b,h}/(n_{b,h}+\lambda)$ ("regularised Mondrian" [@rafe2026]); **HSC-CQR fixed-κ**, score shrinkage with a fixed pseudo-count of one week ($\kappa=168$ h), i.e. a donor–local mixture as in [@jeon2026]; **QShrink-CQR recency** and **Self-CQR recency** (quantile shrinkage and self-calibration with the same recency weights as the shrinkage component; Self-CQR recency falls back to pooled CQR until the weighted own quantile is defined); and **HSC-CQR recency** (Eq. [-@eq:hsc]), the slow component of TTSC, with and without the radius cap.
* *Proposed:* **TTSC** (Eq. [-@eq:ttsc]) and its ablations: *shrinkage only* ($\gamma=0$, i.e. HSC-CQR recency (capped)), *cumulative feedback* (ACI-type level without window, same $\gamma$), *own data one day later* (scores and misses used one day later) and *no cap*.
* *Variants of the shrinkage component (Supplementary Section S5):* **HSC-CQR simple** ($\rho=1$, $\kappa$ by the interval score), **HSC-CQR simple (cov)** ($\rho=1$, $\kappa$ by the coverage objective), **HSC-CQR** (with stage matching and similarity-localised distribution), **HSC-CQR -shrink** ($\kappa=\infty$), **SSC-CQR** and **HSC-SSC-CQR** (Eq. [-@eq:ssc]).
* *Direct probabilistic models:* **QR-LGBM** (LightGBM quantile regression [@ke2017; @koenker1978]), **MLP-QR** (a 256-256-128 multilayer perceptron with pinball loss [@wang2019pinball]) and **MLP-Gauss** (the same network with a Gaussian likelihood, in the spirit of [@salinas2020]); **CQR (MLP)** and **HSC-CQR (MLP)** apply the calibration to the MLP quantiles.

For fairness, ACI-CQR and QShrink-CQR are run both with the interval-score objective and with the window-level coverage objective (suffix "(cov)"); ACI is additionally run with TTSC's weekly objective, sharpness constraint and cap, both as cumulative ACI and as 7-day windowed feedback without the prior ("weekly-tuned"). All strategies are additionally evaluated over their full parameter grids on the target buildings (for transparency only, never for selection; Section [-@sec:ablation]). LightGBM (63 leaves, learning rate 0.05, at least 200 samples per leaf, 0.8 feature and bagging fractions, L2 regularisation 1) and the MLPs (Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch 4096, at most 12 epochs) use early stopping on building-disjoint validation buildings. These are conventional settings, identical for all calibration strategies.

## Outcome measures

For target building $b$ with valid hourly observations over its 56-day window, we compute coverage $\mathrm{cov}_b$, relative width $\overline{U-L}/\bar y_b$ and the relative interval (Winkler) score [@winkler1972; @gneiting2007]

$$
\mathrm{IS}_b=\frac{1}{\bar y_b}\,\overline{(U-L)+\tfrac{2}{\alpha}(L-y)_+ +\tfrac{2}{\alpha}(y-U)_+},
$$ {#eq:is}

a proper score that rewards narrow intervals and penalises misses in proportion to their size.

*Reliability.* The primary reliability outcome is the **under-covered share**: the share of buildings with $\mathrm{cov}_b<1-\alpha-0.05$ (for 90 % intervals, coverage below 0.85), over the window and over the first week (days 1–7). We also report the 10th percentile of $\mathrm{cov}_b$ and the mean absolute coverage deviation $\mathrm{MACD}=\mathrm{mean}_b|\mathrm{cov}_b-(1-\alpha)|$. We also report the 10th percentile of $\mathrm{cov}_b$, the under-covered share in each week of weeks 2–8 (mean over weeks), and the share of buildings with at least one week below 0.80. Per-building coverage is itself an estimate, and misses are serially dependent. As a reference that is identical for every calibration strategy, we construct for every building an *oracle* static interval: its radius is the building's own in-window $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of its CQR scores, so its window coverage is nominal by construction while its daily misses keep the building's real serial dependence. The oracle's daily hit counts are resampled with a 7-day moving-block bootstrap over the building's valid days. The share of oracle buildings that fall below the threshold is the reference for each metric; excess over it is reported with a site-cluster bootstrap confidence interval (BDG2) or a building bootstrap (UCI).

*Sharpness.* The median and the mean over buildings of the relative interval score, and the 99th percentile and maximum of the width ratio to pooled CQR for the same building and seed (tails).

*Operational consequence.* Consider an operator that offers, for every building and hour, a day-ahead capacity headroom $\bar P-U$ below a contracted limit $\bar P$, for example to host flexible EV-charging or heat-pump load, and pays a penalty proportional to the violation $(y-U)_+$ whenever the realised load exceeds the planned upper bound $U$. Up to a constant, the relative cost of a building is

$$
\mathrm{cost}_b(\rho_p)=\frac{\overline{U}+\rho_p\,\overline{(y-U)_+}}{\bar y_b}.
$$ {#eq:cost}

The upper bound of a calibrated central 90 % interval is the cost-optimal plan for the penalty ratio $\rho_p=20$, because the optimal plan is the $(1-1/\rho_p)$ quantile. We report $\rho_p\in\{10,20,40\}$, and the share of **under-protected** buildings, whose upper bound is exceeded in more than 10 % of the hours (twice the nominal 5 %).

## Statistical analysis

The unit of analysis is the target building; a building's metrics are averaged over the seeds in which it was a target. One confirmatory family was declared for TTSC: TTSC against shrinkage only, quantile shrinkage, ACI and pooled CQR on three outcomes (the weekly under-covered share in weeks 2–8, the indicator of any week below 0.80, and the share of failed weekly performance checks), per dataset, Holm-adjusted [@holm1979]. Paired differences are tested by Wilcoxon signed-rank tests [@wilcoxon1945] over buildings and reported with 95 % bootstrap confidence intervals that resample sites (BDG2) or buildings (UCI), because buildings at one site share weather and operation. Interval scores are compared on log ratios with a two one-sided test for equivalence within 3 %. The comparisons among single-timescale strategies (Supplementary Section S5) used the same unit with McNemar tests on under-covered indicators. All other comparisons are exploratory. Standard deviations over seeds describe split variability, not estimation uncertainty.

## Protocol, pre-registration and deviations {#sec:design}

The splits, seeds, metrics, baselines and the interval-score tuning objective were fixed in an internal analysis protocol before any full experiment was run (available with the code on request). After a pilot evaluation, an addendum was written and committed to the repository *before* the corresponding experiments were run. It added the coverage objective (applied to score shrinkage, ACI and QShrink alike), recency weighting with $\rho=0.95$, stage-wise studentisation, the operational outcomes and the noise reference, and it fixed the rule for the recommended single-timescale configuration. That rule selects the strategy with the lowest under-covered share, averaged over both datasets, among those whose median interval score is at most 2 % above that of pooled CQR on both datasets. Every parametrised strategy (pseudo-counts $\kappa$ and $\lambda$, ACI step size $\gamma$) is tuned by the same constrained leave-one-building-out rule on calibration buildings: minimise MACD subject to an interval score at most 2 % above that of the family's pooled limit, over grids $\kappa,\lambda\in\{0,6,12,24,48,96,168,336,672,1344,\infty\}$ h and ACI step sizes $\gamma\in\{0,\dots,0.05\}$; grid-edge selections are reported. To avoid ranking on the data the result is reported on, the recommended single-timescale configuration is selected on BDG2 and confirmed on UCI, on unseen sites and in both cross-dataset directions (nested selection); bootstrap selection frequencies are reported. The decay factor $\rho=0.95$ was set in the addendum and not tuned. Two further variants address deployment questions: own scores used one day later (delay 1d; the forecast for day $h$ uses own scores up to day $h-2$, for gate closures before the end of day $h-1$), and a radius capped at the pooled 99 % score quantile (capped). TTSC was added after these analyses, to address the weekly under-coverage that remained with every single-timescale strategy. It was designed and its window ($W=7$ days, the settlement period) and cap were fixed after inspecting seeds 0 and 1 of BDG2 and seed 0 of UCI; its parameters are selected by leave-one-building-out on calibration buildings only, and seeds 2–4 are reported separately as held-out confirmation. Further deviations, for example the extension of the ACI grid after a code audit, are listed in Supplementary Section S4.
